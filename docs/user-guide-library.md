# User guide: reader and library

Part of the [user guide](user-guide.md).

## Reader & Library

### Library List view

In List view the Type, Language, Released, Status and Tags columns never break a word; a long title wraps between words instead.

### Interactive Reader

Raw and translated text side by side, for proofing and language learning.
- **Word segmentation**: jieba (Chinese), sudachipy (Japanese), kiwipiepy (Korean). Install what you need: `pip install jieba pypinyin` / `pip install sudachipy sudachidict_core pykakasi` / `pip install kiwipiepy`.
- **Ruby annotations**: pinyin over Chinese, furigana over Japanese kanji (Korean is skipped).
- **Click-to-define**: Chinese uses a local CC-CEDICT lookup (downloaded once, then offline). Japanese, Korean and unknown Chinese words use an LLM definition and need an API key.
- **Chapters**: when the title has saved chapters, a chapter button beside the page controls opens the list with a search box and how far each chapter is translated (and reviewed, once you have reviewed lines in it); each shows its translated title beside the original, and a text with volume headings (such as 第二卷 or Volume 2) groups the chapters under them; tapping one opens the page that holds its first line.
- **Pagination** keeps big chapters light. Follow-along playback highlights the spoken line using the Reader's own embedded clip. Reading progress saves when you change pages; playback position is stored separately.
- **Watch page** (a video title's **Watch** or an audio title's **Listen** on the Library): the title's own video or sound with the player on top, the line being spoken shown large under it, and every line listed beside it (under it on a narrower screen); the list follows the sound inside its own scroll area with the coming lines in view, waits a few seconds while you scroll it yourself, and clicking a line plays from there. **Listen** replays the line. If you may edit lines, **Fix Line**, a click on the English or the E key pauses the sound and opens that line's English to fix; **Save and play** (Ctrl+Enter) saves it and plays on from the start of the line, and Esc cancels from anywhere in the fix. Leaving the page with a fix typed saves it (closing the tab asks first); if that save fails, the fix is back in the box the next time you open the page in that tab (it is kept in this tab only, also when a phone puts the tab away). If someone changed the line meanwhile, the save is refused, the new text is shown under **It now says** and your fix stays in the box to check and save again; leaving the page then does not save it, and it comes back the same way, with what the line says by then. Space or Alt+Space plays or pauses (Alt+Space also while you fix a line), L turns Loop line on or off (while it is on, the line being spoken repeats until you turn it off; on this page it starts off each time you open it), and Up and Down move to the previous and next line. Under the line being spoken, three small buttons with no words, a check mark (G, "Looks right"), a cross (X, "Wrong") and a wave (M, "Mixed"), check the line or mark it as your own verdict, each pressed again to undo it (they are the same marks as in Review, and the Marked view there lists them; hold the pointer over one for its name); **Prev. Flag** (P) and **Next Flag** (N) jump to the nearest line you marked or a check flagged. The three marks need permission to edit lines, and a line with no English cannot be marked. Your place is kept in this browser: opening the page again goes back where you stopped, paused, with **Start from the beginning** (over the top of a video, as a small pill). If you may edit lines and the title has lines the Review risk view lists (flagged, marked, unsure or missing a speaker), a **7 lines to check** bar under the player steps through them in place: **Go to the next one** and **‹** (previous) move there without leaving the page, and while such a line is spoken its card says **Check 2 of 7** with the reason, **Fix the English** and **It's fine** (which marks it as looking right, so the count goes down). **More** has Follow along, Read the lines and Save subtitles (on a phone it sits beside **‹ Library** and **Edit**, which opens the title's page as **Work on this title** does on a computer). A sound-only title's line is shown in that large card only, without the player's caption strip or Subtitles choice; Pop out keeps them. A title with no sound or video on this PC shows its lines with links to Follow along and the Reader; if the app could not find out, **Retry** asks again.
- **Follow along** (the button in a title's workspace header, not for comics): a phone-friendly screen that shows the title's lines on their own clock, for reading the translation while you listen to the audio in another app. Start and Pause run the clock, the −5 / −0.5 / +0.5 / +5 s buttons line it up with the audio, tapping a line jumps to it, and a switch shows English or the original. It plays no audio itself and keeps the screen awake while it runs.
- **Listen** (narrated novels only): the Listen section makes the narration of the whole title and shows its progress; when it finishes, the player in Watch / listen plays it, with the lines listed beneath it in the language the voice speaks; the line being spoken is highlighted and clicking a line jumps the audio to it, and **Show** switches the list between Original and English whichever language is being spoken. Once a title has a narration, "Make the narration again" (folded above the player) offers the same voice engine and Voice language choices and replaces the track. It needs OmniVoice, Qwen3-TTS or Chatterbox installed (each has its own card in Diagnostics) and uses the GPU. Listen starts on an engine that is installed. **Voice language** picks Translation (the voice speaks the English lines) or Original language (it speaks the novel's own text, for example Chinese, while the English lines stay for you to read); changing it makes the next run regenerate the clips. Qwen3-TTS and Chatterbox need a reference clip or a voice description for every speaker, so Listen names the speakers that have none and does not start (OmniVoice picks voices for them itself, and saves each pick as that character's voice description, and on its series character, so the voice stays the same when later chapters bring new speakers). The voice speaks two lines of the same voice at once where it can (OmniVoice and Qwen3-TTS), which makes a long book faster. A chapter title written twice in a row is read once. Narration plays at 0.9 of the voice's own speed, for a calmer reading, with the same short gap between lines; **Narration pace** in Settings > System > Advanced changes that (1 is the voice's own speed) without changing the voice's pitch, and needs no new clips. A voice description must use the words OmniVoice knows (male or female, an age, a pitch, whisper, an accent); Listen names a speaker whose description has other words and does not start, because OmniVoice would leave every one of their lines silent. The narration is levelled when it is put together, so every line sits at about the same loudness whichever voice speaks it; a very quiet line is lifted only as far as its background noise allows, and the saved clips are not changed. A line the voice could not speak is left silent, and after the run Listen says how many were and lists the first of them by number. If more than half the lines fail, the previous track is kept and Listen says so, for example "Narration failed on 312 of 400 lines, so the previous track was kept." Without the optional pydub package Listen says "Install the pydub package in Diagnostics > Packages" instead of failing partway. Each line's clip is kept in the title's folder so the next run reuses it; after a run that made a track, clips no line uses any more (left by a changed voice, text or Voice language) are deleted.
- **Change voice** (Listen, beside each speaker's voice): opens the title's Review screen on Change many lines > Voices, scrolled to the speaker cards. Voices is also shown in Guided view.
- **Test voice** (Listen, beside each speaker's voice): speaks that speaker's first line, cut to about a sentence, in the voice the full narration would give them, and plays it, so a voice can be judged before the whole title is made. It uses the engine and Voice language picked above and needs the same engine to be installed; a speaker an engine has no voice for gets the same "no voice yet" message instead.
- **Edit** (the pencil by the pager): shows each saved line on its own with a pencil. Click one, fix the English in the box that opens (Esc cancels), and Save; if the line changed meanwhile, the save is refused and the page reloads; the page reloads with the new text. Turn the pencil off to read the joined paragraphs again. In Edit, the glossary's names (blue) and terms (orange) are highlighted in the English and the original; tap one to change its translation, category and notes, or Delete it (**Delete term…**, then a second press to confirm). Select any English text to **Preview** and then **Replace** it in every line (lines edited since the preview are skipped), or **Add as term** to put it in the glossary.
- **Live while translating**: while a translation runs on the title, the Reader checks about every 10 seconds and reloads the page you are on when new English has arrived, keeping your place on the page. A small note above the text says "Translating, chapter N" and goes when the run ends. It waits while you have text selected or a line open for editing, and only a page of a title with saved chapters refreshes. The text arrives a batch at a time, not word by word.
- **Settings**: text size, line spacing, width, font, lines per page, a Readings switch (the pinyin, furigana or romanization over each word; words stay clickable with it off) and a Reader theme (Match app, Light, Sepia, Dark), separate from the app theme in the header. On a phone the translation sits under its source line instead of beside it.
- **Spoiler-free mode** (Settings, on by default) limits every AI feature (character lookups, recaps, relationship maps, the wiki) to the page you have reached.
- **Universe wiki**: an encyclopedia built as you read (characters, places, sects, artifacts, events), updated on re-extraction and exportable as Markdown.
- **Story tools**: who is this character, relationship map (Mermaid), spoiler-safe recap, explain an idiom or reference; **Ask about the story** answers from the lines loaded so far (an answer the engine blocked or left empty shows as an error). These need an API key.
- **Engine chosen for members**: a member account (and anyone on another device, until an admin there presses **Show options**) sees "The AI engine is chosen for you." instead of the AI engine picker in Words and Story tools, "The voice engine is chosen for you." in Listen, and on a comic's Translate panel "The engine and text detector are chosen for you." instead of the AI engine and Text detector. No engine is sent: the AI tools and comic runs use the default engine, or Ollama when that default is one the account may not use; Listen uses the default voice engine, and comics use the Auto detector. A comic's automatic read-ahead on that Ollama counts as paid work, so it needs a monthly Spending cap like a paid engine.
- **Line tools** (in Review): "Why this?", "Re-write" (feeds the adaptive style profile) and "Re-transcribe this line" (re-runs Whisper on that line's audio only).
- **Re-translate selected lines** (in Review, under History): tick lines, pick an engine and model, and press "Re-translate selected" to redo just those lines with the title's glossary, style, characters and context settings. It replaces their English, hand edits included; the estimate shows before you start.
- **Run 3 times, review differences** (in Review, under History; optional): translates the ticked lines (every line if none is ticked) three times with the title's settings, after you confirm the estimate for all three runs, then lists only the lines where the runs differ. Pick a version per line with a tap or the keys 1, 2, 3 (J and K move, Z undoes the last pick), or press "Keep run N for the rest". Lines all three agree on keep the last run's English. It replaces their English, hand edits included; "Restore earlier English" restores only the English of those lines (a line you edited since is left alone). A pick on a line whose source text changed after that run is skipped.
- **Vocabulary export**: looked-up words export as Anki-importable CSV or a `.apkg` deck (the `.apkg` needs `genanki`; card text shows exactly as looked up, never as HTML). Importing a title's `.apkg` again adds to the same deck and note type in Anki instead of creating new ones.

### Library

- **Baihe unreachable**: the Library says so once, with Retry, and reloads itself within a few seconds of Baihe answering again; a page you have not opened yet in that tab shows the same "Could not reach Baihe" text and reloads when Baihe is back, instead of the browser's error page. Where the top bar shows its words (Jobs, Alerts, Shortcuts and Theme written out instead of as icons, which needs a wide window), it says **Connected** while Baihe answers; it is checked again every 30 seconds and whenever you come back to the window, so it turns into the "Can't reach Baihe" badge when Baihe stops and back to Connected once it answers (a phone shows only the badge).
- **Add box** at the top on a computer (for anyone who can add titles; on a phone, the bottom bar's **Add** opens it on the Add page so the first title stays on screen): paste a link or choose a file, then **Make English subtitles** (the run shows on the Add page), **Add only** (the New title sheet opens with the link already in its Link field) or, before anything is given, **Add a title by hand**. Long titles on the Continue shelf, the cards, the title page header and the side menu wrap onto a second line instead of being cut off with "…".
- **Shelves** at the top of the Library, each hidden when empty: **Ready** (finished titles you have not watched or read to the end, two to a row on a phone, the ones you started first; a card says **New** or shows how far you got, with **Continue at 1:12:40**, **Watch · 2 h 05 m**, **Read · 24 pages** or **Continue reading**, and the whole card opens the title; **Show all N** when there are more), **Needs you** (what only a person can unblock, stopped steps first: **Fix the key** when a key was refused and you are at the PC, else **See what happened** on Jobs; **See episode 1** or **Open the series** for a series whose auto-run is waiting; **Check 7 lines** last, which opens Watch for a video or audio title and Review for the rest; shown only to someone who can edit lines), and one **Running** line (the step that is running, its bar and time left, how many more wait, and **Open Jobs**). A household member who cannot control the PC queue sees **Titles you added** instead of Needs you and Running: their own running and stopped titles, with no buttons, and a stopped one says the PC owner has been told. On a phone, **All titles** jumps to the full list, and the header no longer has its own **Add** button (the bottom bar has it).
- **Continue shelf** with cover art and progress. Tapping a tile's cover or title opens the title; its button says what it does next and opens that place: **Read from page 12** (or **Continue reading**) for a title you are reading; for a title you are working on, **Translate 5 lines** (the Translate step), **Check the 3 lines** (Review, with the count of lines that need a look, worded as on the title page: **Check the line** for one, **Check the lines** while that count is not known), **Export** once nothing needs a look, and **Watch**, **Listen** (both open the Watch page) or **Read** once it is exported. A tile says **Open** and opens the title for a comic, for a title with no lines yet, when the title list has not loaded since you opened the page, and when the page reloaded its data while a search or filter was hiding the title; otherwise a title keeps its label while a search or filter hides it. When a tile is too narrow for its button, and always on a phone, the button sits on its own row under the title. A title on that shelf shows **Continue** instead of **Watch**, **Listen** or **Read** on its Library card; reading history (clearable); the shelf's heading, tiles and More/Less button line up with even padding.
- **Sort** (Library and Series page) offers Date added newest first (the default) or oldest first, Stream / release date oldest or newest first, and Title A-Z.
- **Cards** show a line-progress bar ("12 left" or "40 translated") and a Translate or Review button for the title's next step, beside Open. The quiet button beside Details says **Watch** on a video title, **Listen** on an audio title and **Read** on the rest (novels, narrated novels, comics and subtitle-only titles); List view's row and the Details sheet say the same. Watch and Listen open the Watch page; Read and Continue open the Reader.
- **Covers**: a title's cover can be uploaded by hand (Credits and cover in Edit details) or made from the video: **Make cover from video** takes one frame, and a title with no cover gets one by itself when a video is added to it (from a link, the stream's own thumbnail; otherwise a frame). A cover you set yourself is never replaced. The folder list at **Library > Series** shows the cover of each series' first title that has one.
- **Metadata**: cover, genre, publication status, chapter count, custom tags, private notes, reading/listening time estimates.
- **Stream / release date**: set it in a title's Media > Details (empty = unknown; Baihe never guesses it from the date added). The Library can sort by it (titles without a date last) and **Group by** series, media type or status, with each series' titles oldest first so you can watch in order; a series page has its own **Sort titles by**, and both remember your choice in this browser.
- **Filters**: the Voice actor filter lists titles credited with exactly the picked name ("Li" no longer also lists "Lin Yu"). Removing a title's audio or video that another program holds open, or that Baihe's account may not delete, says so and keeps that file on the title.
- **Dashboard**: totals, translated lines, API calls, estimated spend, cache-hit rate, per-drama costs.
- **Series**: dramas sharing a glossary or characters get a consolidated view. Each series has its own page (`#/series/<id>`), opened from **Series** on the Library page or from "Open series page" in a title's Glossary. It has four tabs: **Titles** (every title in the series with its media type and line count; each opens the title), **Defaults** (below), **Glossary tools** (the term count, a link to the glossary and series instructions, and **Shared glossary** (create a named glossary, for example one per creator, or join an existing one; every series that joined it inherits its terms, such as slang, catchphrases and ASR mishearings, in translation prompts, the glossary check, name fixes after transcription and the transcription vocabulary hint. The series' own term wins over a shared term with the same original text, each term is labelled **This series** or **Shared: name**, removing a shared term (**Remove…**) takes a second press to confirm, and leaving the shared glossary keeps the series' own terms; the person who made a shared glossary, or any admin (away from the PC too), can delete it from the Shared glossary list, which unlinks the series that joined it and keeps their own terms; deactivating a member unlinks the shared glossaries they made from every series, and the glossaries stay for an admin to delete. Remaining places read only the series' own glossary: Retranslate for glossary terms, term spelling and sound-alike suggestions and user backups, which leave shared glossaries behind; the title's **Download CSV** does include the shared glossary's terms, so importing that file on another PC adds them all as the series' own terms) and **Copy terms from another series**: pick a series, see a preview such as "2 new terms will be added, 1 is already there and is skipped", then copy. A term the series already has, with the same original text ignoring case and width, is skipped and keeps its own translation and notes; the source's word replacements come along too (one whose word the series already replaces, ignoring case, is left as it is, up to 500 in all); nothing is removed from the other series, another person's private series is never offered, and a series with more than 2,000 terms can't be copied in one go; **Export TMX** downloads the series' translation memory as a TMX file, and **Import TMX** (for people who can edit lines) adds the pairs of a TMX file, keeping the stored translation of any source the series already has, then saying "Added 3, kept 1" with the real counts; **Name consistency across episodes** (for people who can edit the series) is a read-only check: **Check consistency** lists, for each glossary term and its aliases, every English rendering found in the series' translated lines with line counts per title and a few examples, flags the renderings that are not the glossary's English (for example "Yaloch" where the glossary says "Yalo"), and shows plain numbers you can compare before and after a fix: names with more than one rendering, off-glossary lines and lines still flagged for a missing glossary name. Nothing changes until you press **Redo … lines…** on a flag and confirm: that sends those lines, title by title, to the stronger engine picked in Settings, so it costs like any run) and **Settings** (**Rename series**, refused when another series you can see has the same name ignoring case; **Delete series…**, PC only, first lists what goes and what stays: the titles are kept and become "No series", while the series glossary, series characters, instructions, translation memory, dismissed suggestions and learned style profile are deleted with it. A series holding anything needs DELETE typed; an empty one a second press. It is refused while a job runs for any of its titles, and no files are touched). A title with no series gets a private series of its own the first time you save a glossary term or series instructions, so a single novel can have a glossary; pick a series, or "New series…", in its Glossary to share one between titles. The Series page's **Defaults** tab holds default title settings (translation engine, the Translate toggles and the Transcribe tuning; never API keys, Groq transcription or Thinking, which only someone allowed to use paid engines may switch on for a title): a new title in the series starts with them, a title moved into it gets them only for settings it has no value for, and **Apply to all titles in this series** previews, per title, what would change before writing it ("Only fill settings a title hasn't changed" by default, where a value equal to the app's default counts as unchanged, or "Overwrite every title's settings"); it changes only titles you may edit and is refused while a job runs for any title in the series. The same tab has **Run new episodes automatically**: turn it on with a spending ceiling (pre-filled with $1.00, in US dollars, for all episodes it runs together; it does not need the monthly cap), and each episode in the series that has audio or video but no lines (other than one tagged "Test copy") goes through Transcribe, Translate, Auto QC and Export on the Jobs queue, one episode at a time, as you, with the title's saved settings. Start the queue on the Jobs page for anything to run. It stops after the first episode for you to check the subtitles and goes on when you press **Continue**; it also stops, with the reason, when an episode fails (fix it and Retry on the Jobs page, or Continue to skip it) and when the ceiling is spent (raise it to go on). Each translation run is capped to what is left of the ceiling and is refused up front when its estimate is above it. Turning it off withdraws episodes that have not started. The same tab has a **Speakers** setting: **Solo creator (one speaker)** is for a series where one person talks alone, so speaker detection no longer splits them into several speakers. If you enrolled that creator's voice in the series characters, lines in that voice get that name and lines in any other voice stay blank; with no enrolled voice every line gets the one speaker. It applies the next time speakers are detected or re-assigned from the saved detection, and lines already labelled are not changed; **Auto** (the default) keeps every voice found. The New title, Edit details and Glossary series pickers now list every series you can see, one-title and empty ones included.
- **Where settings come from** (a title's Translate tab, opened from the title page's side panel): lists each title setting a series can set, with its value and whether it is the title's own, the same as the series default, or from Settings because the title has none. A series default or a profile is copied onto a title when you apply it, so a profile's values show as the title's own.
- **Search and bulk actions**: a global search over every drama's original and translated lines; bulk status, **Series…** (put every selected title in an existing or new series, or take them out; the library admin's bar, like status and lists), delete (typed confirm) and translate (skips dramas without a key, lines, or already running). Deleting a title, alone or in bulk, waits for a file still uploading to it, pages still being added, or novel text or an EPUB still being saved (bulk delete skips it as "an upload is in progress"); someone else's save or job that lands on a title just deleted says it may have just been deleted instead of failing with an error.
- **Browse by series**: on the Library, open **Series** and choose **Browse all series as folders** (or go to `#/series`) for a file-explorer style list: every series as a folder with its title count, sorted by name, with a search box once there are more than six. Click a series to see its titles; the breadcrumb on a series page leads back to the list. **Streamers**: give a series a streamer name in its Settings tab (**Streamer**, optional; it suggests names already in use, and a name differing only in case joins the existing one). Once any series has one, the list opens on the streamers instead, each with its series and title counts and a **No streamer** folder last; a streamer opens its series, and a series opens its titles (Library > Streamers > Chion > series). Clearing the name moves the series back to No streamer.
- **Series in the details popup**: a title's details show its series (linked to the series page) or "No series (no shared glossary)". **Add to series…** / **Change…** picks an existing series, creates one ("+ New series…") or removes the title from its series; it works from away for an admin, and members don't see the button.
- **Last capture in the details popup**: after the browser extension saves a chapter into a title, its details show the line the capture ended with (for example "Saved 69 of 69 pages…") and when, so you can read it after the extension popup is gone. **Earlier captures** lists up to four before it; only the newest five are kept, and a title never captured by the extension shows nothing.
- **Make test copy**: in a title's details, **Make test copy** makes "<name> (copy)", tagged "Test copy", with its own copy of the files, lines, speakers and comic pages, in the same series, so a rerun on the copy never changes the original. It copies the files (check free disk space), leaves out translation notes, emotion tags, reading progress and the source link, and is refused while a job runs on the original. It works from away too for an admin; members don't see the button.
- **Library tools** (page; the breadcrumb at the top leads back to the Library): export all translated titles as a zip (Organize tab), per-drama export package (with a manifest of what was included), translation setting profiles (pick one on New title; edit, rename or make one the default here), voice bank (Organize tab; **Add voice…** (at the PC only) adds a voice straight from a 1 to 30 second clip on your computer, with a name, language (Chinese, Japanese, Korean or English), voice engine and optional transcript, so a voice can be kept without any title; each row has Play, **Details** (what the clip says, notes and language, and **Replace clip** at the PC, after which the cursor is in "What the clip says" to fix it for the new clip; titles that already used the voice keep their copy, and applying the voice again gives a title the new clip, which its next narration speaks with), Rename and delete; with more than three voices a search box and a language filter appear; the voice bank is shared with everyone in the household, except a voice an admin ticked **Private** in its Details: members don't see it in the list, the voice picker or its clip, and can't apply it (admins and the PC owner see it, marked Private); someone who can't see the title a voice was saved from doesn't see that title's name or what the clip says, and gets no Details button for it; the **Save to voice bank** box on a speaker says "Shared with everyone in the household"), series, costs and reading history. It has six tabs: Organize, Voices (every person with a voice print: how many samples went into it, how well each linked speaker matches it, and, at the PC, **Retrain** (rebuild the print from the speakers linked to that person), **Clear print** (forget the print, keep the person) and **Remove** on a speaker (unlink it and rebuild without it); each asks for a second press. Individual samples are not stored, so a retrain rebuilds from the speakers still linked and says how many older samples can't be rebuilt), Activity, Backup (Back up with the library, database only and just your stuff, then Restore, then the automatic backups (schedule) and their copies), Storage (how much space is used and free on the drive, the cleanup scan with quality presets (Archival, Balanced, Minimal; only regenerable files are removed. Balanced and Minimal also remove the voice-only copy a "Separate vocals first" run leaves beside the audio; after that, hearing one line again uses the full audio until the next full transcription. Replacing or removing a title's audio removes that copy by itself), unused voice clips, the Trash and temp files) and Data folder (what is using space in Baihe's data folder; the program's own files sit in one closed "Program files (protected)" row). Away from the PC, Backup, Storage and Data folder are left out, and Voices is read-only. The page opens on the tab you used last. The Data folder view never follows a link or junction when it counts a folder: the folder's size is what is inside it, and the folders the links lead to are shown apart as "Linked folder, stored elsewhere" with their own size, and as "plus N in linked folders" next to the total. Those are not counted in the folder's size or percentage, and a link, or a folder holding one, can't be moved to Trash from here. In library > dramas each title also shows its size by kind: originals (source video and audio, uploaded and replaced media, page scans, the novel files), raw transcripts, line history (the snapshots in the database) and rebuildable files (what the storage presets may remove).
- **Typeset all comics again** (Library tools > Organize, at the PC, and only once the library has a comic): redraws the pages of every comic that has translations, the same as Redo > Typeset only on each title, for example after an update changed how English sits in balloons. It is free (nothing is read or translated again), keeps your English and box edits, asks to confirm, goes one title at a time on the Jobs page (cancel any time; running it again is safe), skips titles with no translations or one that is busy, and ends with how many titles and pages it redrew and how many failed.
- **Backup and restore**: a database-only snapshot or a full zip with media, streamed to disk; restore validates the zip first and needs a typed confirm. Signed-in browser sessions from Sources are not included. - **Backup and restore**: a database-only snapshot or a full zip with media, streamed to disk; restore validates the zip first and needs a typed confirm. Signed-in browser sessions from Sources are not included, nor are API keys, members' own keys and sign-in sessions: enter the keys again after a restore. A full backup is read back and checked once written (the job shows the check's progress, and Cancel stops it); one that fails the check is removed and the job says "The backup failed its check", leaving earlier backups as they were. Automatic backups are on by default (database only, daily; an install that never chose keeps them on after the upgrade, and turning them off is a saved choice) and keep one copy a day for 7 days plus one a week for 4 weeks. They are set up on Library tools > Backup (the schedule, folder, "Back up now" and the copies sit there next to Restore); a search for "backup" in Settings links there. Before each automatic backup Baihe checks the backup drive: with less free space than 1.2 times the expected copy (the last copy's size, or the library's for the first one) the run is skipped, the settings page shows a warning, no old copy is deleted, and it is tried again a day later. See `docs/runbook.md` section 3. Baihe records which version last opened your library and refuses to start if that was a newer version than the one you are running, since an older build could misread the newer data A library a source checkout opened counts as the next release, so an installed copy older than that release refuses it too. Upgrade Baihe, or restore a backup made before you updated (later copies carry the newer version's mark too), from the backups folder in your Baihe data folder. Restore refuses a backup made by a newer Baihe in the same way and says so, and so do importing titles from a backup and restoring one title from an automatic backup copy. If `library.db` itself is damaged, Baihe doesn't start and says how to put back the `library.db` from your newest full backup; it never tries to repair the file. Importing titles with "From a backup file…" (Library tools > Backup) adds each series as a new "… (imported <date>)" series, unless you tick "Add each series to the one of the same name here": then a series whose name matches one you may add titles to joins it, its new characters and glossary terms are added, a character with the same name stays yours with the two voice prints combined (a print from a different voice model is left out), and anything else you already have stays as it is. Two more tick boxes appear when the file has them. One adds the file's voice bank (a full backup zip only; an entry whose clip you already have in your bank is skipped, even if you renamed it). The other takes the file's Translation and Preferences settings. API keys are never in a backup, and folders, App updates and the System page stay as this PC has them; the result says to enter the keys again. Titles imported from a backup made before lines had ids keep their translation notes on the right lines. Imported titles bring their spend history with them, so the Library's "spent" total includes what they cost in the library they came from; a call this library already has is not counted twice, and calls dated this month count toward this PC's monthly cap (the month's total in Settings then says "includes $X imported"). The database inside a backup (and the per-person backup) holds each title's source link as it was imported, query included, which can carry a site's sign-in or download token; treat backup files as private. A backup's `manifest.json`, the export zip and support reports never carry it. A backup holds the sign-in accounts (no passwords, sessions or device tokens). A rebuilt PC gets those accounts back only if the backup holds them and the library you restore into had no accounts yet; a library that already has accounts keeps its own: each restored title then goes to the account with the same email (or to the PC owner when there is none), private titles stay private, the restore summary says how many titles changed owner, and the backup's followed live channels pass to the PC owner with automatic checks off. Restoring one title from an automatic backup copy matches its owner the same way (by email, or the PC owner when there is no match or the copy holds no accounts), keeps a private title private, and the result says how many owners changed. Media of titles added since a database-only backup, and folders that backup lacks, stay in the previous library beside the library folder; Storage > Previous library shows its size and deletes it. Reset library keeps a copy of the last library there too.
- **Keep original files** (a title's Media > Add media tab): when on, the storage scan presets never remove that title's source audio and video, uploaded files, raw novel text, raw transcripts or page scans; the export zip puts them in the title's `originals/` folder; and deleting an item from Trash that came from the title's folder says so in the confirmation. They keep using disk space. Changing it needs the same permission as editing the title's details.
- **Open original page**: every page shows the title's source link without its `?query` part, which can hold a download token. On the main PC the button opens the link exactly as it was imported or typed instead (a video link may need its `?v=`); away from the PC it opens the cleaned link. The full link is never sent to household or remote accounts.
- **Translation versions**: every translation run is saved with its engine and model; compare versions side by side and activate one (the current one is snapshotted first).
- **Undo**: line snapshots are taken before risky edits (a force re-translate, an applied merge, a re-transcribe, a shorten, a cleanup, activating a version); restore from Records → Line history (the 20 most recent are kept; all but the newest 3 are stored compressed, and they restore the same way).
- **Drama IDs skip numbers after a deletion, on purpose.** Lines, glossaries and versions reference a drama by id, so reusing one could re-attach stale rows or conflict with an older backup. The Library shows a separate sequential **#** column.

### Discover and titles

- Discover has two tabs, **Find a title** and **Add titles**; the Catalogue tab (a search of titles you've saved locally, with "Load starter titles" and "Import to library") is currently hidden. Discover > "Find a title" > "Find on official platforms" builds site-scoped search links across known platforms. It deliberately never asks a model to suggest titles, since a model invents plausible ones.
- Baihehub search translates your query to Chinese first and is best effort; if it comes back empty you get a link to its own search page.
- Add a title by URL (a listing page), by hand, or by **bulk import** of up to 10 tag/ranking listing pages (title, author, tags, audio-drama flag; never content). A review table always comes before saving, with a "paste the page text" fallback for JS-only sites. Members can add titles and bulk import on a phone too (up to 200 each); reading a page or listing from a link needs the Import from a link right, while pasted text does not.
- **Known sites** (`known_sites.py`): a curated directory of official platforms for Chinese baihe, Korean GL and Japanese yuri, browsable in Discover's "Open a site or explain a page". No aggregator or scanlation sites.
- "Open a site or explain a page" opens a site in a new tab (most sites refuse to be shown inside another page), or has the PC read a public page in a language you don't read and write step-by-step help for it.

### Manhua and Scanlate

Detection is heuristic and surfaced for review. A speech bubble is an enclosed light region that doesn't touch the page edge and holds lettering clear of its outline (a white panel, screentone or an empty white shape is not one); the ML detector's balloon and text boxes for one balloon become one region; panels are found by gutter analysis, also on grey scanned paper; regions are read panel by panel, right to left for Japanese and left to right for Chinese and Korean; long webtoon strips are sliced at whitespace gaps with a small overlap; regions are classified (bubble, narration, sign, SFX, thought), their text direction is read from the lettering, and font style is sampled.

**Open folder** (every title's header, in the Guided and All tools views, on the main PC only) shows the title's files in File Explorer: `library\dramas\<title number>` in the data folder, which is where a comic's pages, `reader_cache` and Scanlate files live too. When Baihe runs as a background service it can't open a window, so the button shows that folder's full path with a **Copy path** button to paste into File Explorer, in a notice that fits the bottom of a phone screen.

Comics are translated on the Comic page, not in the Workspace's Translate, Review and Export stages (those show "Comics are translated in Scanlate" with an **Open in Scanlate** button). The page's **Translate** button opens the Scanlate panel. The Comic page loads a smaller JPEG copy of each page (made the first time the page is opened and kept in the title's `reader_cache` folder) so pages appear faster over a slow link; the Editor still uses the full-size file.
**Read translated** (on the Comic page) takes a title, its language and a chapter link, makes the comic, saves the pages and opens the reader; Baihe translates from the page you are on while you read, translating straight away with no glossary step. On the Library card the comic shows "Translated 4 of 12 pages" once translation has started. It does not yet load the next chapter by itself.

While you read a comic that already has translated pages, Baihe translates the next 3 pages in the background so they are ready when you turn the page. It does this only for a title you have already started translating, uses the engine you picked, and stops when you leave the page. With a paid engine it only runs while a monthly cap is set in Settings, and it counts against that cap.
**Page menu**: hold a finger on a comic page (or right-click it) for **Save picture**, **Share picture** (where the browser can share files, as phones do) and **Set as cover** (needs upload rights); it uses the picture as shown, typeset or original.

**Read marks**: reaching a comic chapter's last page marks it read, shown as a tick in the chapter list; a bookmarked chapter shows a star. **Next unread** in the chapter row jumps to the next chapter without a tick (after the last one it goes back to the earliest unread). In **Chapters & pages** (the ⋯ button): **Mark read** / **Mark unread**, **Bookmark** / **Remove bookmark**, and **Mark this and earlier read**. The marks are your own: each sign-in account keeps its own ticks and stars, and at the PC without sign-in they are the PC's. Marks made before accounts had their own went to the PC and to each admin account as a starting point. Deleting an account deletes its marks.

**Reading filter and unread count** (Library): the **Reading** box beside the status and quick filters shows only titles that are **Unread**, **Started** or **Finished** (by how far the household has read, watched or listened; 99% counts as finished, as on the Continue shelf) or that **Have new chapters**; a comic's card shows how many of its chapters have no read mark of yours ("3 unread chapters"; the count follows the account you are signed in with).

**Double page** (View settings, in the one-page-at-a-time modes) shows two pages side by side, the first page on the right for right-to-left reading. It is set to Automatic by default, which turns it on whenever the window is landscape and at least 900 px wide, and it can be forced on or off. **First page alone** keeps the cover by itself so the pairs after it line up; a page that is already wider than tall, or a tall strip (at least twice as tall as wide), is always shown alone. Arrows and taps turn a whole spread, the counter shows both page numbers, and the choice is saved with the title's other view settings.
**Already in your Library**: while you type a title or paste a link in the New title form, Add a Comic or the Sources "New title…" box, Baihe lists the titles you can see that have the same name (ignoring capitals and spacing) or the same link, each with a link to open it. It is only a warning; you can still add a separate title.

**Add a Comic** (on the Comic page) makes a comic title and fills it from a pasted **Link**, picked **Images**, a **CBZ** file or a whole **Folder** (a folder's pictures are added in name order, subfolders included; images, CBZ and folder need upload rights), and sets the **Reading mode** the title opens in. A CBZ that unpacks to more than 20 GB is refused.
1. Upload page images or PDFs (PC only; optionally "Slice tall strips").
2. Pick, when the title has chapters, this chapter or all chapters. Advanced holds the **AI engine** and **Translate captions on the art**, and **Text detector**: Auto (the ML detector `ogkalu/comic-text-and-bubble-detector` once it is downloaded, else the free OpenCV heuristic), OpenCV heuristic, or ML model (downloads once; needs `transformers` and `huggingface_hub`). The OCR backend is the one in Settings > Advanced > Offline, paths and limits > OCR.
3. With no page uploaded yet the panel shows only the upload; the translate controls appear with the first page. **Translate all pages** detects, reads, cleans, translates and typesets each page, skipping pages that already have text; **Redo this page…** (beside Translate, on phones too) and **Redo all…** (or **Redo chapter…**) replace a page's text after a confirm. **Translate captions on the art** (off by default) also paints out large lettering drawn on the artwork and sets the English in the same colour and glow, in a regular face when the original strokes are thin (needs PaddleOCR and the LaMa-manga model; about 5 seconds per caption). A caption cut by the page break is translated as one line and the English split between the two pages when one run (**Translate all pages**, **Redo all…** or **Redo chapter…**) translates both. The English starts at the size of the original lettering and is set smaller when it, or a word, doesn't fit its bubble. Cleanup erases only inside a bubble's outline, so the outline and the art around it stay. If a redone page can't be typeset, the reader and the export show the original page, not the old translation. In a narrow balloon a long English word is split with a hyphen rather than shrunk to tiny letters.
4. Read the result with the **Typeset pages** view setting, then Export ZIP or PDF. The export follows that setting (off exports the original pages) and says which pages are untranslated.

**Glossary and characters.** Scanlate translates with the title's series glossary and the series characters: each character's pronouns (the Gender field, such as she/her) and voice notes are sent with every page, so a character keeps one gender and manner of speaking. A title with no series is translated without them. The Scanlate panel's Advanced section and the "Comics are translated in Scanlate" page both link to **Glossary and characters**, where you edit them.

A text box can be fixed on its own: its English, position and size, font size and style, its kind, and its colour (left unset, the typeset text is white on a dark balloon and black otherwise), with undo of the latest changes on that page (the last 50). The comic editor is the page `#/comic/<id>/edit?page=N`: pick a text box on the page, drag it, drag a corner to resize it, or change its English, font, kind, size and colour in the side panel and press **Apply**; each drop or Apply is one change. **Kind** sets how the box is typeset: `bubble` and `thought` fit the text to the balloon's outline, `narration` and `sign` to the box, `caption` paints out lettering on the art and re-letters it, and `sfx` is lettered across the box (the automatic run leaves sound effects it finds alone; one you set to `sfx` here is translated and typeset). **Draw** (left of the page) lets you drag a rectangle for a new text box, up to 200 on a page, which is then selected; a new box is a `bubble` until you pick another **Kind** and press **Apply**; **Brush** (under Draw) drags the same rectangle over a bubble the run missed, then reads and translates it (two **Undo** steps: the read, then the box); **Delete box** removes the selected one; **Undo** takes back the latest (a deleted box returns as it was), **Show Original** shows the page without the typeset text, **Previous** and **Next** change page, and **Save page** re-renders the typeset image. **Editor** on the Comic page opens it at the page you are reading. With **Text** on, tapping a text box on the Comic page opens a sheet with that line's original (**Copy** copies it) and its English; **Save page** saves the change and re-renders the page, without leaving the reader. **Show Original** on the Comic page switches the current page between its typeset and original image. A change is refused, never saved over, when a job or another edit wrote the page after you opened it; reload the page and make it again. Undo is refused the same way when something else changed the page after your edit. Re-render the page to see the change in the typeset image.

The Chapters & pages sheet can also list the source's chapters and import one. On a title saved before chapters were recorded, which reads as one list, open the sheet at the first page of a chapter and press **Start a new chapter at page N** (the name is optional); the pages from there to the end of that run become a chapter you can pick, jump to and read on its own.
Limits: the CV detector misses borderless or irregular bubbles; text is erased with the LaMa-manga model when its weights are downloaded (on the GPU when Settings allows GPU use and the region clearly fits beside what is already loaded there, otherwise on the CPU; Models in memory lists it while it holds GPU memory), otherwise with OpenCV's inpainting (weak on patterned backgrounds); text is horizontal only; OCR crops slightly inside the bubble border, so check the source text. With Tesseract, vertical Japanese and Chinese read far better when its vertical language packs (`jpn_vert`, `chi_sim_vert`, `chi_tra_vert`) are installed. With RapidOCR, vertical columns are read right to left and furigana beside them is left out of the source text.

## Metadata & site tools

### Browser extension

A Chrome/Edge extension that sends the page you're reading into Baihe and draws the translation over it. It also reaches pages an adapter can't (`blob:` images, site-unscrambled readers, signed-in pages) because your own browser already did that work. Turn it on in Settings > Integrations > "Browser extension", load `extension/` unpacked (`chrome://extensions` > Developer mode > Load unpacked) and paste the token. It talks only to `127.0.0.1`. Pages of one title are translated one at a time, each with the context of the page that finished just before it: that follows the order captures get their turn, not page order, so a page sent out of order (or racing another) reads the wrong neighbour's context, and one that waits over 30 seconds behind a slow engine is translated without context. When several pages are sent at once, each is drawn on the reader as soon as it is done instead of when the whole batch is, and the next page is read while one is being translated. When the bridge starts, it loads the balloon detector and the text eraser in the background (only if they are already downloaded), so the first page doesn't wait for them; this keeps them in memory while Baihe runs. Details: [`browser-extension.md`](browser-extension.md). On another computer in the household, make a token under Settings > Sharing and devices, load the same folder, and in the extension options enter the household address and the token.

**Baihe Grab (Android).** The Android app has one icon. Baihe Grab opens from a long-press on the Baihe Studio icon (Baihe Grab), from a link to another site tapped in Baihe, or from Android's Share on a link or images. Open a comic site in it, press Grab, and the chapter's pages go into Baihe like the extension's Auto title. In Settings enter the server address (https) and a phone token that starts with `baihe_dt_`: a member makes it in Settings > Extension tokens per computer, and an admin makes it at the PC in the same card ("Add a computer for the admin account") and copies it to the phone, since it is shown once. In Grab, More > Translate this page shows the site you are browsing in English with Google's free page translator (it stays on while you move between pages; More > Show original page turns it off), and it switches itself off when you press Grab, Chapters or Quick read, so those always use the site's own page; the translator is Google's, so the page's text is sent to Google while it is on. Grab does not load the common ad networks at all, whatever DNS the phone uses (a Pi-hole only helps on its own network with Private DNS off). Grab scrolls the page first so lazily drawn pages load, and leaves out reaction emotes, avatars and ads (by their markup, or small square pictures repeated at one size that are far smaller than the chapter's pages), and the server translates each page as it arrives. **Chapters** grabs this chapter and then follows the page's next-chapter link (same site only) for the number you pick, up to 20; it stops at a chapter that sent nothing, and the button reads Stop while it runs. **Quick read** (second row) reads the page without saving it: the PC translates and redraws the pages and Grab shows them one after another, with nothing added to the library and the temporary pages deleted when you close the reader. In that reader, pinch or double-tap to zoom (up to four times; drag sideways while zoomed), the volume keys scroll most of a screen down or up, and the screen stays awake. When you are within three pages of the end and the site has a next-chapter link, Quick read fetches and translates the next chapter into the same reader, one chapter ahead at most; it stops when the spending limit is reached, and the tick box in Grab's Settings turns it off. It uses your engine and spending limit like a saved Grab, and a page the PC could not translate is left out. On a novel chapter (a page of text with no comic pictures, Chinese, Japanese or Korean) Quick read translates the text on the PC in parts and shows it as plain text that is kept nowhere; the volume keys scroll, and Cancel stops the rest. A normal Grab on a novel page does not save it: it says to use Quick read. A link to another site tapped in the main Baihe app opens in Grab instead of Chrome (Google sign-in pages still open in Chrome). In the phone app, the Menu has an Open Baihe Grab link (needs a build from 1092). A video taken full screen in Studio turns the phone sideways and back when you leave it. A Studio | Grab strip at the top of both screens switches between them: each keeps its page, and a grab that is running keeps going while you look at Studio. In Grab, More > Add to queue lines up this chapter (and the ones after it, up to 20) to be saved after the grab that is running; the queue runs one after another until you press Cancel or Stop, and More > Queue and history opens a page of its own: what is waiting (Open page, Remove, Start the queue) and the last 50 runs with how each ended, each with Open page to go back to where it started and, for a Quick read, Read again, which asks the PC for the Quick read again (it costs no tokens while the PC still remembers the page, 14 days) and keeps nothing on the phone. Cancel (in Grab or in the Quick read reader) cuts the requests to the PC at once instead of waiting for the page being redrawn, and tells the PC to stop. The PC remembers each translated Quick read page and novel part for 14 days (in a size-limited cache that is not a saved series), so reading or grabbing the same chapter again costs no tokens; a page that came back with an untranslated line or a warning is not remembered. In the Quick read reader, press and hold a page and choose Send a report (with an optional note) when a page came out wrong: the redrawn page, what the PC read and translated on it, the picture's address and the chapter go to the PC, and an admin finds it in the problem reports list. A Quick read is not queued. It is a release build (not debuggable), signed with the same key as earlier builds so phones update in place, from the manual Android APK workflow.

### The Sources page

**Updates** (in the menu, under Find) is one list of everything new, newest first: the new chapters of tracked series (several chapters of one series are one row) and your own followed Live channels that are live now or have a stream scheduled. Tap a chapter row to open that series in Sources, or a channel row to open Live; it works the same on a phone. Nothing is marked read or dismissed there: dismiss chapters in New chapters, check channels on the Live page, and press Refresh to reload.

Sources has three tabs. **Find** has a switch, "Search by title" or "Paste a link". Search asks each set-up source for the title (each source is paced, so it can take a minute, and Cancel keeps what already came back); picking a result opens its chapter list, where you choose chapters to import into a title or save as CBZ and can start tracking the series. Paste a link previews one page without importing anything (a "Check" reads it once and shows what it found), then imports it. "Will this site work?" reads one page and says whether it can be imported; where Baihe has the site's terms on record it shows them as information and never refuses because of them. **New chapters** lists tracked series, the new chapters a check found, and "Check now". "Auto-import into" picks the title that receives a series' new chapters; with it off they are only listed. A title that a tracked series is linked to there shows a badge on its Library card ("3 new chapters") for the new chapters a check found; the badge goes when you dismiss them in New chapters. "Stop tracking…" asks first. When a site dies, open the same series on another source and use "Move a tracked title here": "Compare chapters" shows which chapters match (they stay as they are), which are only on the old source (they stay saved) and which are new, and "Move here…" points the tracked title at the new source. Saved chapters and translated pages are never deleted, and a move that fails changes nothing. **Manage sources** is one list of folds: Sites (every site, including ones that only work through the browser extension, and adult works), Timing and cache (pacing between requests, advanced timing, the saved-pages cache with "Clear cache…", developer options), Network (proxy, which is the only proxy Sources uses: the Windows or environment proxy settings are ignored; and saved page layouts per site) and Activity.

**Saved manga** (in Library tools) lists the chapters saved as CBZ files and opens them in a built-in reader that has the same **Double page** and **First page alone** settings as the comic reader, saved per series in this browser; the files also work in any comic reader. The **Assistant** page answers questions about the app's code and logs and is read-only: it suggests fixes but never applies them.

### When a source or a lookup fails

A Sources action that fails says why: the site is limiting requests (try again in a few minutes), took too long, could not be reached, refused this connection, needs a sign-in or purchase, or changed its pages so the adapter can't read them. In a chapter import or save a chapter whose page can't be read fails on its own and the rest go on; a comic chapter with no pages is reported, not counted as imported. Test now on a paused source says how long the pause has left and keeps the last result. "Will this site work?" no longer says a built-in adapter supports a page it could not load. Research online names Gemini's reason too: the key's request limit is used up, the key was refused, Gemini is down, a safety filter withheld the answer, or the answer was cut off; when Google refused the request before searching, no search is counted. Research online uses Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite or 3.8 Flash (Google is retiring 2.5 on 1 December) and needs a paid Gemini key, because Google gives free-tier keys no Google Search on these models; a free-tier key is told so before anything is asked. Google gives a paid key 5,000 free searches a month, then about $0.014 each. When Google says a model's free requests for today are used up, Research online stops using that model until the next day and asks you to pick the other one. "Create + fill with AI" (metadata autofill) now uses Gemini, so a Gemini key is enough.

### Metadata romanization

Credits keep the original script and gain a romanized companion: 一半山川 displays as "Yiban Shanchuan (一半山川)". Personal names are romanized; studios and platforms use their established English name where one exists (晋江文学城 as "JJWXC").

### Fetching from JS-heavy sites

Some sites (baihehub, Fanjiao) build pages with JavaScript, so a plain fetch returns an empty shell. `page_fetch.py` handles it in three layers: it detects an unrendered shell and says so; with the `playwright` package installed (Diagnostics > Packages and installs > Packages, or `pip install playwright`) it re-fetches with a real browser: an installed Chrome or Edge, Playwright's own Chromium, or the program named by the `BAIHE_BROWSER_PATH` system environment variable. No browser download is needed when Chrome or Edge is installed; Diagnostics > Setup shows the package and the browser separately (the browser install is on Packages and installs); and manual paste (copy the page text into the app) always works.
