# User guide: subtitles

Part of the [user guide](user-guide.md).

## Translation

### Translation engines

| Engine | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `claude` | Paid per token (API key from console.anthropic.com, billed separately from a Claude.ai subscription) | Best tone and character voice; novel reference with prompt caching. Model picker in Workspace > Translate (Sonnet 5.5 default, Opus 5.5, plus older Sonnet and Opus kept for saved presets); see `CLAUDE_MODELS` in `engine_backends/pricing.py`. |
| `deepseek` | Paid, far cheaper than Claude | Strong on Chinese; a good default for context-aware, glossary-aware work. |
| `gemini` | Paid, cheap (Flash-Lite tier); key from aistudio.google.com | Strong on Chinese/Japanese. Translation only: transcription still uses Whisper. Lineup changes often; see `GEMINI_MODELS`. |
| `openai` | Paid per token; `BAIHE_OPENAI_KEY` | GPT models (default `gpt-6-luna`; also `gpt-6.1-terra` and `gpt-6.1-sol`) over Chat Completions; a saved `gpt-5`, `gpt-5-mini` or `gpt-5-nano` runs as `gpt-6-luna`. Newer GPT-6+ models appear in the picker after Diagnostics > Models > Model status with "Also offer models … that this app doesn't know yet" ticked, costed at a high ceiling ($5 in / $40 out per 1M tokens) because their real price is unknown; see `OPENAI_MODELS`. |
| `ollama` | Free, uses your hardware | Local Gemma 4 via [Ollama](https://ollama.com) on your GPU; free and private. The default 12B model is about 8 GB and fits fully on a 12 GB GPU. TranslateGemma 12B is also listed, suggested for Japanese and Korean. Ollama's hosted "-cloud" Gemma models are not free: they are billed per token by Ollama and counted toward your monthly cap. The model pickers (Translate and Live) also list every chat model you have pulled in Ollama (embedding models are left out). |

Claude, DeepSeek, Gemini, OpenAI and Ollama can tag speakers for novel-narration mode and take the novel reference; an engine that can't follow instructions tags everything "Narrator". **Chunk and tag** with no AI key set still cuts the pasted text into lines of up to 200 characters and marks every one "Narrator" without any AI call, and says so; add a key in Settings and run it again to tag speakers. Its **Model** is a list of the engine's models (for Ollama, the ones installed) with "Engine default" first; an engine with no list keeps a typed box. "Replace the N existing lines" is a switch.

**Prompt wording by content type.** The translator's prompt follows the title's content mode: audio dramas and ASMR get spoken-line rules (one breath, delivery cues kept), novels get prose rules (English a native novelist would write: subjects supplied, clauses joined or split, idioms by sense not word, standard double quotation marks), streamer VODs get live-caption rules, and video dramas get subtitle rules. The notes on a series character (speaking style, relationships) are passed to the translator as voice notes for that title's named characters, so keep them short.

**A narrated novel reads as prose.** The Reader joins the chunks back into paragraphs with the English as the main text, and Review leaves out the fake timestamp and the speaker.

**Context from recent lines.** Translate > More options has "Context before" and "Context ahead" (number fields; the defaults are 10 before and 6 ahead, and 30 and 15 for a narrated novel, whose batches also end at paragraph breaks; 0 turns it off). They show LLM engines how the preceding lines were already translated, so pronouns and relation-only references stay consistent across batches. Consistency across separate VODs of one streamer comes from assigning them to the same series. After a translate run, the title's Translate form starts from that run's style, style note, English variant, model, batch size and context sizes, and the Queue, the Library batch, **Redo these with …** and Review's "Retranslate these" repeat them.

**Reply check.** After each batch on an AI engine, Baihe checks replies that carry every id but look wrong: two lines merged into one, text shifted onto a neighbouring line, a duplicate, or source-language script left in the English (script from glossary terms and quotes is allowed). Those lines are asked again once, in at most two extra requests per batch, and a reply is replaced only if the new one fails fewer checks. A line that still looks wrong is kept and flagged "check: may be merged or shifted". The extra requests are billed like any other and count toward the spending cap. It does not run in Reflect mode or Translate by sentence. By default (Settings > Translation and keys > Translation style > "Check translations for merged or shifted lines" = Auto) it runs only for engines other than Claude and Gemini Pro, which rarely need it; choose On to check every engine or Off to never check. With **Translate by sentence** on, a sentence the model returns in one piece (or repeats in every part) is split across its parts by length, and those parts are flagged "check: ... split across the parts by length" so you can check each matches its audio.

**Reasoning (Claude Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna).** Settings > Translation and keys > Translation style has "Claude Sonnet 5.5 reasoning" and "GPT-6 Luna reasoning", each Default or Less. Sonnet 5.5 is Default unless you change it; Luna is Less unless you change it. Less makes a Translate run on that model reason less: on one 90-line Mandarin stream chunk, Luna went from 67 s to 24 s and under half the cost with about the same quality, and Sonnet 5.5 from 27 s to 12 s and 40% less cost with slightly worse slang and nicknames. It applies only to those two models (Terra, Sol and other Claude models are unchanged) and to Translate runs (including the Claude bulk batch), not to Live, Compare or the review passes.

**Source match check.** Off by default (Settings > Translation and keys > Translation style > "Flag translations that don't match their source"). When on, a finished translation run compares each line's English with its source using the `bge-m3` model on your Ollama server (run `ollama pull bge-m3` once) and flags lines that look shifted, skipped or about something else, with the note "Source match: the English reads unlike its source line (similarity 0.xx)". It changes no text, uses no paid engine, skips lines whose source is under three characters, never replaces another flag, and does nothing if Ollama or the model is missing. It does not catch small meaning errors such as a flipped negation. It runs after Translate from the Workspace, not after the off-peak bulk run.

**Bulk batch replies.** A finished Bulk batch (Claude or Gemini) checks each reply the way a normal run does: a reply that is empty, JSON, reasoning text, still in the source script or far too long for its line is not written; the line stays blank and is flagged "check: the model's reply for this line was unusable" with the rejected text in the note so you can copy it back, and the batch summary counts it as rejected. Setting the reply check to Off turns this off here too. If saving a finished batch fails before any English was written (a locked database), it is tried once more from the same results without logging the cost twice; one unreadable reply no longer costs the replies next to it. Two cases are not covered: if the app stops while a finished batch is being applied, or the provider reports the batch failed or expired, the paid output may need to be bought again.

**Reflect** (the Translate stage's Reflect toggle) sends its fixed instructions once as a system block instead of repeating them in each prompt; on Claude that block is cached, so the three passes and later batches re-read it at the cached price.

### Translation guide (style, terms, and notes)

Configured per drama in the Workspace's Translate stage.

In Simple, Review's tools under the line list show only **Check** and **Fix** (a link to another tool opens Check), and the bar above the lines leaves out **Compact rows**, **Go to #** and **Shortcuts**; Full has them all.
In Simple, Review opens on **Need a look**: the unchecked lines a check flagged or Whisper was unsure of, riskiest first, with a **Need a look N** | **All M** switch, "F of M look fine" beside it (lines with no English count as neither) and an **i** saying why lines are listed. Each card says why it is there (its flag, or "Whisper was unsure of this line (N% sure)"), a glossary or name flag adds **Open glossary**, and **Looks right, next** (or R) marks the line checked and opens the next card, with **Undo** above the list. Under the cards, **Read them all** switches to All, a line says how many lines have no English yet with **Show the untranslated lines**, and **Mark the rest as checked…** (asks first) marks the other unflagged lines checked the way Mark episode reviewed does; it waits, and says why, while a line Whisper was unsure of is still listed or the list runs past one page. The filters, the step buttons and Replace… are under All. On a phone the queue shows one card at a time with "1 of 3 to look at" beside the switch ("F of M look fine" is left to the line under the cards), the next two lines and their reasons, **See the lines around it**, and **Skip for now** and **Looks right, next** in the bar; the step tabs, their main button and **More**, the Simple / Full switch, Open folder and the source link fold into one line beside the title ("Step 4 of 5 · Check") that opens and closes them. A link that names lines (Ctrl+K, a flagged-lines link, **Open all N lines**) opens on those lines instead.
In Simple, the Run tab leaves out its **Advanced options** (the tuning and the speaker-count and re-detect controls); the basic choices, Transcribe and the estimate stay, and Full shows everything.
A title panel sits beside the lines on a wide window (under them on a phone, and beside the steps while there are no lines yet) with tabs: **Video** (only when the title has a source video; a plain player), **Translate** (the engine, model, style and English variant Translate opens with, plus **Change** and **Estimate cost**, which open the Translate page), **Glossary** (**Open glossary**), **Episodes** (the title's series with how far each episode is) and **Export**. Once an audio or video title has lines, its Simple page also shows a thin progress bar ("6 of 10 lines translated") and the first eight lines (original and English, wrapped in full, "Not translated yet" where English is missing) with **Open all N lines**, which opens Review on all lines. The step tabs run across the top, above the bar and the lines, and wrap onto more rows on a phone. Pressing a step on an audio or video title keeps the tabs above it, with the step itself in place of the lines, and the open step's tab is marked.
A novel title opens on its saved chapters with **Translate**, **Add chapters**, **Glossary**, **Export** and **Read** above the list, and without the Media and Review tabs; each button opens the matching stage under a slim header (back link to the title) with a row of **Steps**, **Media**, **Translate** and **Export** links under it, so you can move between stages without switching to Full; the same goes for the buttons on the comic Simple page. On a novel title the main button is the next step: **Translate** once the title has lines (with saved chapters but no lines yet, **Translate** runs the same steps as **Continue** in the link box: lines, speaker tags and the glossary scan, then the glossary review), and **Review** (above Translate) once every line has English. Pasting novel text on a title with no saved chapters saves it as that title's chapters (split at the chapter headings), and the attached count is chapters, not paragraphs. **Read** always opens the Reader. Once translation has started, the link box becomes **Add chapters from a link** with an **Add from link** button: it imports the chapter, appends only the new chapters to the translation text and adds lines for that new text after the saved ones (only the new lines get speakers tagged; every saved line, including ones you edited, merged or split in Review, is left exactly as it is); it never starts a translation, so press Translate afterwards. If the translation text is not exactly the first saved chapters (pasted, edited or cut) it stops and says so instead of overwriting or guessing; add the chapters in the Source stage then. An EPUB is split into chapters by its own table of contents (a document the contents doesn't name continues the chapter before it), and a blank title, author or cover is filled from the book's metadata. Once the title has lines, each chapter row also shows how far it is translated (Not translated, a count such as 12 of 40 lines translated, or Translated) and a **Read** link that opens the Reader at that chapter's first page. A chapter that has some English also has **Translate again**: it shows how many lines will be translated and the most it can cost, then runs the normal translation on just that chapter with the title's saved engine, glossary and style. Lines you edited by hand are left alone unless you tick **Include the N hand-edited lines**, and the old English stays in the line history. When the glossary scan has finished, a **Cast found in the text** card lists the people it found, with the pronouns it read from the text and a short note on who each is: tick the right ones, change any pronouns, and press **Apply to characters**. Nothing is saved before that, no model call is made for it, and pronouns that are already set (on the title's character or the series character) are never replaced; a new name is kept as a series character when the title has a series. A chapter that has some English also has **Translate again**: it shows how many lines will be translated and the most it can cost, then runs the normal translation on just that chapter with the title's saved engine, glossary and style. Lines you edited by hand are left alone unless you tick **Include the N hand-edited lines**, and the old English stays in the line history.
A novel title with no lines yet also has a link box: **Import from link** imports the chapters, splits them into lines, tags speakers and finds glossary terms, then stops at **Review glossary and translate**, which opens the Translate tab with the scan's review already open (**Review proposals** reopens it if you cancel): tick the names, then **Add N terms and start translation** adds them and starts the translation, and **Next: Review** opens the result. The link box also has a **Skip the glossary review** switch, off by default and remembered per browser: with it on, the chain adds the proposed terms that are not in the glossary yet and have a translation, then starts translating without stopping, and the title page then leads with **Review** once it finishes. If a step fails, its error shows and **Continue** picks up from where the title is.

An audio drama or video title has a **Simple** / **Full** switch in its header (on a phone, in the Title actions menu). Full is the workspace with the Transcribe, Translate, Review and Export tabs (the first one is called Media on a comic). Simple, opened from a link with no stage, shows five step tabs across the top (Add, Transcribe, Translate, Check, Export), each with a one-line note of where the title stands (for example "18 of 30 lines left") that opens the matching stage. The first step not yet done is outlined and its action is the one main button: **Add media**, **Transcribe and translate**, **Translate 18 lines**, **Check the 3 lines** (the Check note says how many lines need a look; on the Review page the button opens Need a look on its first card) or **Export** (once nothing needs a look). Beside it stay **Add media** (when it is not the main button), **Redo transcription** (once there are lines) and **Glossary** (on a phone these three are behind a **More** button, and the tabs show only their names, three to a row); the title's episodes (the titles of its series, or just itself, with how far each one is) are below. The choice is remembered in this browser, Simple is the default until someone picks Full, and a link that names a stage always opens that stage. Simple also trims the stages: in Review, a line shows its start time only (no end time, characters-per-second number or dub file name; the number still appears on a line flagged for reading speed), and the active line's buttons are Play, Edit details, Improve translation, Mark checked and any flag button (in Need a look, **Looks right, next** in place of Mark checked), with Why this?, Split and Merge left to the line's **⋯** menu and their keys; Add media leaves out Only this part, Import subtitle file and Keep original files; Translate says its engine, style and English variant in one line with a **Change** button that opens the four choices, Estimate cost and Re-translate, and leaves out More options and the Characters and Reference novel tabs; Export offers only Video with a subtitle track and Subtitle file. Full shows everything. Novels and comics have the switch too: a novel opens on its novel page (Simple) and Full is the stage workspace; a comic's Simple page has **Open in Scanlate**, **Add pages**, **Glossary** and **Export** above its **Chapters** (each with its page count and how many pages have text; one opens the reader on that chapter) and the **Titles in this series**, and Full is the workspace. Each of the three groups remembers its own choice. On the Simple page of an audio or video title that has audio but no lines yet, **Transcribe and translate** transcribes it with the saved options, finds names for the glossary, then stops at **Review the glossary**; translating starts from there.

The **Instructions** tab beside **Chapters** holds the title's instructions and its **Term conventions** (one per line, saved as the last section of the same text); both go into every translation, and the conventions also guide the terms the glossary finds in the novel. **Draft from novel** has the title's engine read a sample of the novel and write a style instruction (genre, tone, point of view, voice) into the box for you to edit; nothing is saved until you press **Save**, and it is one paid call on that engine.

The Translate stage has four tabs, one screen each (the last one you used is remembered per title): **Translate** (engine, model, style, the estimate and the Translate button, More options, the notices about failed batches or missing glossary names, Bulk batches), **Glossary** (the panel below, with its language packs), **Characters** and **Reference novel** (an existing English translation of the novel, used to keep names consistent). A running job's progress stays above the tabs whichever one you are on. A link can open one with `#/drama/<id>/translate?tab=glossary` (or `translate`, `characters`, `novel`). In every tab only the first card opens by itself; the others are folded and remember how you left them.

- **Style presets** change register and pacing: audio drama (spoken, contractions), novel (literary), subtitles (instant comprehension), manhua (concise bubble dialogue).
- **Term policies**, per term: keep as pinyin (沈清疑 → Shen Qingyi), hybrid (云隐宗 → Yunyin Sect), translate meaning (听雨阁 → Listening Rain Pavilion), keep + note, or contextual (姐姐 → "jiejie" or "older sister"). Terms are categorized (name, sect, title, honorific, place, artifact, concept...) and grouped by policy in the prompt.
- **The Glossary panel has seven tabs**, and it remembers the last one per title: **Terms** (the table with a search box, a sort choice and a count of matching terms, Add term), **Find terms** (Suggest terms from the transcript or the novel; on a title with saved chapters a chooser next to it scans one chapter instead of the whole title, and the button then reads **Scan this chapter**, and **Scan all chapters** scans each chapter in turn and adds the terms it finds without overwriting yours (a term must appear at least twice in a chapter; ones you ignored stay out; one call per chapter, so a paid engine is billed per chapter; it runs on the server and carries on if you leave the page), Import or export; an imported file saved as GBK, Big5, Shift-JIS, EUC-KR or UTF-16, such as a CSV from Chinese Excel, is read in that encoding), **Names** (Find possible names, the Find similar and Suggest spellings panels, Check names across all titles), **Packs** (language packs and VTuber names), **Instructions** (project and series instructions, each with its own Save and a character count), **Replacements** (whole-word swaps for the series, such as "thee" to "you": every line a translation run writes has them applied, ignoring case; lines already translated are not changed, and a title with no series gets one of its own on the first one you add) and **Re-translate** (re-translate the lines the glossary affects). A scan outlives the page: leave and come back, or reload, and a banner on the Translate stage says "A glossary scan is running" or "Scan finished: N proposals to review" with a button to open it, whether or not "Review glossary before translating" is on. A Suggest spellings answer is kept the same way until the app restarts. Suggest terms from the transcript also lists nicknames and slang, and a term whose meaning the model only inferred from context shows Low even when it repeats, so Select all High leaves it for you to check.
- **Auto-extract terms** proposes terms with a category and policy from a novel or a drama's own transcript; everything goes to a review table first. Given both the original novel and an English translation, it extracts matched pairs. Only terms are stored, never passages. A proposal is kept only if its original text appears in the excerpt the engine read, a character's pronoun counts only when the engine quoted the line that shows it (otherwise it reads "unclear"), a word the engine found ambiguous (such as 阿姨, aunt or nanny) shows its other reading beside the suggestion, and the terms already in your glossary are given to the engine with their English so new terms stay consistent. A passage whose reply the engine cut off is asked again in two halves instead of losing its terms. Terms join the series glossary. A proposal also lists other spellings of the same name that the passage itself shows ("also written"); applying it saves them as the term's aliases, added to any you already had.
- **Import / export glossaries** as CSV, TSV or JSON (a two-column term/translation sheet works; category, policy, enforce_exact and notes columns are picked up when present). **Download glossary as CSV** and **Download glossary as JSON** (in Import or export) both carry every column, aliases and banned renderings included, and either file imports back, into this series or another.
- **Enforce exact** (the glossary's "Exact" column) does a hard find-and-replace after translation for names where drift is unacceptable.
- **Translation notes** flag what didn't survive the crossing (set phrases, puns, allusions, honorifics); they are stored per line and export as a Markdown appendix.
- **Language packs** (Glossary > Packs) are built-in lists of honorifics, address terms, family words and set phrases for Chinese, Japanese and Korean, plus shared drama terms. Each title turns packs on or off; "View entries" lists a pack and can add one entry to the title's glossary. Settings > Language packs shows every pack with its entries and notes, which are on by default, and lets you open a pack to read it; a title follows the defaults until it gets its own choice, and the title's own glossary always beats a pack entry. Only the entries whose source text occurs in a run go into the prompt, at most 60, the ones used most first. Four VTuber packs (`ja-vtuber-core`, `ja-vtuber-names`, `zh-vtuber-core`, `zh-vtuber-names`) are off by default.
- **Find similar** (a glossary row's button, which opens it on the Names tab) looks through the title's source lines for other spellings that sound like that term. Nothing is added until you confirm "Add as alias". **Find possible names** (under the glossary table) looks for names written several slightly different ways and offers to make a term from them. Both are free and offline, and change nothing until you confirm.
- **Address terms** (Glossary > Names > **Find address terms**) counts the common forms of address in the title's source lines (姐姐, 老师, さん, お兄ちゃん, 오빠, 선배 and similar; a form heard at least twice, not already a glossary term or alias), with counts and example lines. For each one type the English rendering, or tick **Keep as-is (romanize)**, then **Add N terms** saves them as glossary terms in the honorific category so the same form is translated the same way across the series. Free and offline; nothing is added until you press Add, and a form already in the glossary is left alone.
- **Aliases fix the transcript.** After a transcription finishes, every spelling you saved as an alias of a glossary term is rewritten to the term's own text in the title's source lines (only whole names, and never a spelling two terms share). Titles of the same series share the glossary, so a spelling you confirm once is fixed in that creator's later titles too. Translation is told about them as well: the glossary it receives lists each term's aliases ("also written in the source as ..."), so a spelling still in the lines (an imported file, a comic page, a spelling you confirmed after transcribing) is read as the same name. The stored source text is not changed by this, and only aliases you confirmed are sent.
- **Suggest spellings** (a glossary row's button) asks this title's engine, once, how the English name you type may have been written in the transcript. Only guesses that really appear in the lines are listed, with counts and example lines; nothing is added until you confirm "Add as alias". It uses one engine request (it counts toward the monthly cap and can be cancelled). If the model's answer can't be read, it says so and changes nothing. **Check English names**, in the same panel, is free and lists lines that mention the name but whose English doesn't use the name you typed; it only reports.
- **Genre guidance** (toggleable): pronoun clarity, kinship terms as intimate address, no softening of romantic content.
- **Dialogue check** (novels): a line whose source has quoted speech or a speaker tag (道, 說, 问...) but whose translation lost the quotation marks or the tag goes back once, with the other suspect lines, under the same cost cap and Cancel as the other re-asks. A reply that fixes it replaces the line; one that doesn't leaves the line as it was, unflagged.
- **Glossary check** runs after every translate batch. A line whose source holds a glossary term (or alias) must have the term's English in its translation. A line that misses goes back once to the same engine, with the missed names listed first, and only a reply that now has the name replaces it. A line still wrong is flagged in Review ("Glossary: <term> missing") and the Translate stage offers **Redo these with <stronger engine>** (the engine picked in Settings), limited to those lines and counted against the monthly cap. A local Ollama model also gets the batch's names next to its lines, where it pays attention to them. The re-ask costs one extra request only for the lines that missed. A Gemma 4 stream run with the plain stream prompt checks only the names and terms that prompt lists, so an ordinary word in the glossary (a food, clothing or custom entry) is not demanded or flagged.
- **Retry these N lines**: when a run has failed batches, the notice on the Translate stage lists the failed lines and says whether they are still empty ("still untranslated") or kept an earlier engine's English ("kept their earlier translation"). The button translates exactly those lines with the engine and model in the form, replacing the earlier English on them (a line-history snapshot is taken first). If the failed reply was cut off, it uses half the batch size.
- **Episode brief** (Media > Details tab > More details, under Running episode summary): before a translate run over the whole title, one extra request reads the whole episode and writes a short brief (who is who, register, recurring terms, ambiguous lines) that every batch of the run carries; it applies to subtitle and novel-narration titles. The brief is kept in this box so you can edit it: an edited brief is used as written, and clearing it makes the next run write a new one. A wrong brief colours every line, so check it. Settings > Translation > Read the whole episode before translating turns the extra request off (on by default; it costs one request per episode and delays the first batch). A title in a series with episode numbers also gives each batch the summaries of the episodes before the previous one (never a later episode), besides the previous episode's summary.
- **Generate summary** (Media > Details tab > More details, under Running episode summary) writes the episode summary from the translated lines with the engine you pick there. It fills the box; press Save to keep it. A translate run that writes no summary says why in its Jobs result ("Summary skipped: Ollama not reachable; choose an engine in Settings"), and the engine used for it is set in Settings.

### Adaptive translation style

Lines you rewrite in Review are recorded as before/after pairs. After 8 or more, they can be analysed into a style profile that is shown for review before it is injected into future translation prompts. Scoped per series when one is assigned, otherwise global. With household accounts, a profile reaches only admins, the PC owner and the person who learned it.

### Review queue

**Riskiest first** (a chip beside Least confident, shown when some unchecked translated line has a flag or a low Whisper confidence) lists those lines most worrying first. The order adds up what the app already stored: the kind of flag (a changed number or name, a source mismatch or a content block outranks a timing or reading-speed flag) and how unsure Whisper was (a score under 0.8 adds a little, near 0 adds the most). Checked and untranslated lines are left out, and lines never flagged or scored are not listed, so a clean-looking line can still be wrong: this is a reading order, not a verdict.

**Next line to check** (beside the Flagged arrows, shown while some translated line is unchecked) opens the next translated line after the one you are on that nobody has marked checked, on any page, and says "No more unchecked lines." at the end. It follows the line order; it does not rank lines by risk.

Under the line list, Review's tools are four groups named by what they are for: **Look for problems** (the four AI checks, each with its own Bulk switch, and their results; Flag lines for review; changed source, gaps, untranslated lines and pacing, with the count of things to look at on the group; Compare transcription; Audio tags), **Change many lines** (Fix flagged lines, Re-translate selected lines, Run 3 times, Structure, Re-split long lines, Merge short lines, Shorten overlong, Fix common errors, Re-time, Re-transcribe selected lines, and Voices: a reference clip, voice bank entry and voice actor per speaker, for cloning), **Records** (notes, versions and line history, Compare versions, Notes export, Edit tendencies, Learn my style; under Line history, **Compare saves side by side** puts any two saves, or a save and the lines now, in two columns with only the changed lines and the changed words marked, J and K or the Previous/Next change buttons step through the changes, and **Use this** on a line copies that side's English into the lines now, with **Undo last**) and **Finish** (Burned subtitle preview, Mark episode reviewed). Simple shows only the AI checks, Flag lines for review, Fix flagged lines and Mark episode reviewed; a button that needs another tool, such as **Transcribe this gap** on the waveform or **Re-transcribe selected** on the selection bar, adds that tool for that title and opens its group. A message that names Records → Line history (the Undo notice after a split, merge, add or delete, a refused Undo, clearing reading-speed flags) has a **Line history** button that opens it, in Simple too. The last group is remembered per title, and `#/drama/3/review?tools=problems` opens a group (`problems`, `change`, `records`, `finish`; a link or bookmark that names one of the old tabs opens the group that now holds it). On the waveform, a dashed "No line here" region marks a stretch with no line, including the one after the last line to the end of the audio when the speech check heard speech there ("speech detected" when the speech check heard speech); the last line's end can be dragged out into that stretch; "Transcribe this gap" adds lines for it, and the "Gaps" chip next to Flagged jumps to the next one (the arrows beside it step to the previous or next). **Add lines for talk-over** (Change many lines > Re-split long lines, beside Re-assign speakers from saved detection) looks, in the saved speaker detection, for a stretch where a second speaker talks over another's line. If that speaker's own next line starts within 1.5 seconds after the stretch (or part way into it), the line is started where their voice begins, so it overlaps the other line; if they have no line near, a blank flagged line is added for you to type into (Whisper cannot hear the quieter voice, so it is not transcribed for you). It is saved to line history and needs Detect speakers to have run. "Find shot changes" on the waveform looks for cuts in the video in the window on screen and marks them with orange ticks; a line edge dragged to within 8 pixels of a tick lands on it, `,` and `.` move the playhead to the previous and next tick, and a title with no video shows an error instead. On a title with a video, **Frame timing** opens the picture at the line's first and last frame side by side: ◀ frame and frame ▶ (or ← and →, Shift for ten frames) step the edge you are on, and `,` and `.` jump it to the previous or next shot change. On a wide screen, a title with a video keeps the video and the waveform pinned in a card beside the line list, so you can adjust a line's edges on the waveform, read the lines and watch the video without scrolling; the lines scroll on their own, together with the toolbar and Find and replace, in a section under the video on a screen at least 1400 px wide (the video is smaller on a screen under 860 px tall), so the video, the checks and the steps stay in view; on a smaller screen the video card sits beside the lines as before. In a line's **Edit details**, **Speaker** is a dropdown of the title's speakers with **No speaker** and **Add new speaker…**, which turns the field into a box for typing a new name. The Add line form's Speaker works the same way. With lines ticked, the selection bar's **Set speaker…** gives every ticked line one speaker (pick one, or type a new name) and marks them as set by hand; **Undo** puts back each line's old speaker while those lines are unchanged. In the Translate stage's Characters tab, **Swap speakers…** on a speaker trades its lines with another speaker's (for two labels that are the wrong way round), and **Undo swap** reverses it. Its **Sample** column plays short clips of each speaker, to hear whose voice a label really is: **▶ 1**, **▶ 2** and **▶ 3** play one clip each (hover one for its length and words), and **Find clips** cuts up to three clips of 3 to 12 seconds from the title's audio (from the detected speaker turns, or the speaker's lines), the same clips Review's Voices offers as a reference clip, saying why when none is long enough. A title with no audio has no Sample column. **Pop out** in the player row keeps the player floating: in Chrome or Edge on this PC it becomes a small always-on-top window you resize like any window; in other browsers, on a phone, or on a page that isn't HTTPS or localhost, it floats over the page instead, where you drag its top bar to move it, resize it from the corner handle (drag it, or focus it and press the arrow keys) or with the − and + buttons, and **Return player** puts it back. The floating size is remembered. On a phone the video player starts closed ("Show player" opens it; audio-only titles keep it open) so the lines are on the first screen, and the Gaps chip reads "Gap 1 of 2" with the arrows beside it. Scrolling there shrinks the sticky bar to the filter chips so more lines show, and it stays shrunk until you scroll back to the very top (it no longer flickers while you scroll). On a laptop-sized screen (under 900 px tall) the player's seek bar and the waveform sit under the sticky bar and scroll away with the page, so the bar keeps only the filters and the play row and several lines stay visible. A translation that stops on its engine's errors before writing any English ends as Failed with the first problem, not Done, and pressing Translate again right after Cancel says the previous AI request is still finishing until the engine's own answer comes back or times out. The stage's Next link drops to its own line on tablet-width windows instead of covering the Export tab.

Review > Look for problems > "Flag lines for a second look" asks the translation engine to flag only the already-translated lines worth a second look (possible mistranslation, unresolved pronoun, uncertain name, idiom). Flags are saved, can be filtered with the "Flagged" tab, and clear when you edit the line or dismiss the flag.

Each translated line can be marked **checked** (the row's "Mark checked" button, the phone sheet, or R): a Checked badge sits on the line, the **Unreviewed** chip lists the translated lines still waiting, and editing the English clears the mark. A mark is refused (409) if the English changed since you read it, so a translate job finishing under your cursor never leaves a Checked badge on text you did not see. Importing a subtitle file that fills every line marks the title translated, as Translate does, and once every line is translated the Translate stage's primary button is **Next: Review**.

When Whisper transcribes a line, Baihe keeps how sure Whisper was of it. A line it was unsure of and that nobody has checked shows a **Shaky** badge with the score (on a phone the selected line spells out what the badge means; on a wider screen an (i) beside it does), and the **Least confident** chip (shown only while a title has such lines) lists them worst first, so you can start where errors are likely. Editing a line's source text, or marking it checked, takes it out of that list; lines from other engines, imported subtitles and titles transcribed before this have no score.

**Mark episode reviewed** (Review > Finish > Mark episode reviewed…, two steps) marks every translated line that is not flagged or already checked as checked, and records the lines you left untouched as approved translations in the series' translation memory, so they appear as Accept suggestions on later episodes. Use it only after reading the whole episode: lines you never read teach their mistakes too. Flagged lines are skipped, a source the memory already holds keeps its entry, and a repeat adds nothing. A title outside a series is marked checked only. Untouched lines are not added to the learned-style samples, which are made of edits only. In Translate > Advanced, **Use approved translations as examples** (off, this run only) adds the series' closest approved pairs to the prompt as examples; a line is never shown its own answer, and whether it improves quality has not been measured.

If a line you are editing was changed elsewhere (another tab, another person, a running job), saving says so; **Reload** shows the other change, says which field it replaced your typing in, and keeps what you typed in the other fields, so you can save again (also when the line is outside the current filter or search). Find and replace saves an open edit of a matched line before applying. An unsaved edit brought back after leaving Review never overwrites a change made elsewhere to a field you typed in: saving it shows the conflict first. Splitting a line together with its translation is refused when the translation changed since you opened the split. A Line history restore's preview counts lines whose flag or SFX mark alone would change, and the restore is refused when a line it would remove got a note after its warning was shown (the warning then counts it). Ticked lines are cleared after re-segmenting, re-splitting, an AI merge or a restore.

A line marked "Source changed" had its source text changed (by an edit, a re-transcription or Fix flagged) after it was translated: its English is kept but may no longer match, and Translate and Export show how many such lines remain. In Review checks, "Retranslate these" translates exactly those lines again with the Translate stage's starting options, and "Keep English for all" (or "Keep English" on one line) keeps the English as it is and removes the mark.

**Fix flagged** re-translates the flagged lines in batches and, on an AI engine, tells it why each line was flagged and shows the English it has now. If the GPU crashes while Fix flagged or Compare transcription is hearing lines, the lines not yet heard are heard again on the CPU. A line whose new English is the same as before stays flagged (nothing was fixed), and the result counts those lines. When hearing a line again changes its source text, the AI is still told the reason you flagged it for, and if no new English lands the line is marked "Source changed" with that reason kept in its note. Fix flagged, the AI flag pass, the consistency check, emotion tagging, translation notes and AI re-segmentation are refused with "the monthly cap is used up" once this month's Spending cap is spent. After a Compare transcription run, **Flag lines that differ** flags the lines the other engine heard differently from the saved text (below about 60% alike), with both versions in the note; it leaves lines that already have a flag or were edited since, and clears its own flag on a rerun where the lines now agree. **Re-split long lines** offers "More" and "Sentence by sentence"; for Chinese and Japanese titles "More" is greyed out, with an Install button, until the word splitter (jieba, or sudachipy for Japanese) is installed. The one-click "Split long lines" button uses the fresh-transcription limits (about 12 seconds or 40 characters).

### Emotion-aware translation

Lines are tagged with an emotional register (14, including sarcasm, dry humour, suppressed anger) plus an intensity, and the tags are folded into the next translation run for charged lines only. Tags also map to TTS rate and pitch for dubbing.

### Streamer VODs and series

For a streamer, "English title" and "Original title" are the translated and untranslated stream name, and the **Source URL** field keeps the original link (auto-filled for URL downloads, and shown without its `?query` part; see **Open original page**). Assign every stream to one **series** to share its glossary, style profile and named characters. Speaker labels (`SPEAKER_00`) are not stable across recordings, so a named character lives at series level: add it from the Characters section, then pick it from the dropdown in later streams. Typing a name does not add it to the series unless you tick "Remember {name} in this series". If you renamed or merged speakers after detection, Remember still builds the voice print, from the detector speakers that sit cleanly on that name's lines (at least 10 seconds); a speaker whose lines share a detector speaker with someone else gets the name but no voice print.

### Raw novel

A title created as a novel starts in novel-narration mode, so its Media stage leads with the text to translate and the raw-novel upload below is only shown once there is a text to prime from.

One upload of the original-language novel (Workspace > Transcribe > Run > "Raw source novel") feeds transcription priming and, with an English reference translation, matched-pair glossary extraction.

An uploaded or pasted `.txt` or `.epub` with chapter headings (第1章, 第1話, 제1화, "Chapter 1") is listed chapter by chapter in the title's saved chapters, with any title page or blurb before chapter 1 as its own entry; a text without headings stays one block. A `.txt` saved in GBK, Big5 or Shift-JIS is read in that encoding whatever the title's script setting. Imported chapters leave out the site's navigation rows and "remember our address" lines, EPUB text leaves out furigana readings and the contents page, and chapter OCR joins each paragraph back together (no spaces between Chinese or Japanese characters). A chapter a site splits over several pages ("下一页", `1_2.html`) comes in as one chapter: importing its address reads its later pages too (up to 10 pages in all), and following next-chapter links joins each chapter's pages when you import them from the review; if a later page can't be read, the pages read so far open in the review instead of being saved. `python tools/novel_import_quality.py` scores these imports on saved examples. Open a chapter in the Source stage's Saved chapters and press **Rename** to change its title: the saved text, the chapter list and the matching line change together, and the previous text is kept beside it as a `.before-edit` copy. **Move earlier** and **Move later** put the chapter one place up or down: the saved text, the chapter list and the chapter's lines move together, each line keeps its translation, and the old order stays in the line history ("before moving a chapter"). Pressing either asks you to confirm first. A move is refused while a chapter import is running or half done, when the lines no longer match the saved chapters word for word, or when the lines have their own times. The `.before-edit` copy is overwritten by each move or rename, so it holds only the latest change. If the translation text is not an exact copy of the saved chapters it is left as it is, and the page says so.

## Transcription & OCR

### Transcription accuracy

The first audio or video you add to an empty title makes the Run tab transcribe it with Whisper; pick "I have a transcript" on the Transcribe stage to paste text instead. A pasted line that matches nothing Whisper heard gets a time guessed from the lines around it and is flagged "Timing uncertain", so Review shows which times to check.

Whisper often mishears proper nouns in Chinese without it showing. In order of value:
1. **A real transcript.**
2. **Prime it with names.** The series glossary feeds Whisper's `initial_prompt` automatically (only about the last 224 tokens influence decoding, so the glossary's nouns go first, then a bounded excerpt of raw novel prose; `.txt`, `.md` or `.epub`).
3. **Whisper model**: `large-v3` is the default, by the owner's choice (it read better on his streamer comparisons without adjusting any Whisper setting). That is a readability preference, not a measured accuracy gain: Baihe's own VOD test showed no difference in character errors (turbo 16.4 against large-v3 18.7 on the first 5 minutes, noise about 2 points). It is ~3GB and about twice as slow as `large-v3-turbo` on a CPU, which stays the fast option (~1.6GB). The default is the same on CPU and GPU; titles that saved a model keep it. `medium` and smaller are hidden from the picker everywhere except Live, Developer Mode included; a title that saved one shows it as "older choice, hidden" with a button to switch to large-v3. See "Which Whisper model to pick" in `docs/asr-experiments.md`.
   - **Before you press Transcribe**, one line says what will run ("This run will use: Whisper · Whisper large-v3 · GPU on"). It says GPU off when the setting is on but the CUDA libraries are missing, because Whisper then runs on the CPU, and GPU on, none found when the setting is on but no NVIDIA GPU was found. With a Qwen3 ASR backend that skips Whisper, the Whisper model and the Whisper-only switches are off ("Not used with Qwen3 ASR") and the line names Qwen3-ASR 1.7B. For stream titles keep Separate vocals first off. The Jobs Details panel says "Engine that ran: Qwen3-ASR (Whisper model setting not used)" for such a run.
   - **Whisper fast mode** is off by default. It decodes several chunks at once (about 2-4x faster on a GPU, more GPU memory) but can give fewer, longer lines and more errors (our test: 44 lines against 150); try Compare transcription first.
4. **Wider beam search** (8-10): costs time only.
5. **Breeze-ASR-25** (Chinese titles only, opt-in, about 3 GB, downloaded on first use; the picker shows it disabled, with the reason, for Japanese and Korean): a Taiwanese Mandarin Whisper model. In two tests on whispered speech it gave steadier results than large-v3, but it still misses short fillers and can write a credit line over a quiet stretch, so compare it with large-v3 on your title before relying on it.
6. **Japanese-only models** (Japanese titles only; the picker shows them disabled, with the reason, for Chinese and Korean): `ja-1.5b` (anime and games, about 3 GB, needs a GPU, licence not declared) and `kotoba-v2` (kotoba-whisper v2.0, about 1.5 GB, much faster than large-v3). kotoba gives line timing only: install the Qwen3 forced aligner (Diagnostics) and Baihe refines the times; without it the Whisper line times are kept and the job reports that timing was not refined. If a transcription is interrupted (a crash, a power cut, Cancel), starting Transcribe again on the same title with the same file and settings continues after the last finished two minutes of audio instead of starting over; any changed setting or a different file starts from the beginning, and fast mode, Groq, Mixed languages and the Qwen3 speech-detection backends always start over.

The Transcribe stage (Media on a comic or novel) has three sub-tabs (the last one you used is remembered per title): **Add media** (where the lines come from, upload or URL, import a subtitle file, Keep original files; the media file box also takes a dropped file, and a dropped `.srt`, `.vtt`, `.ass` or `.lrc` lands in Import subtitle file), **Transcribe** (language, model, Detect speakers, the Transcribe button and the last run, then Advanced options, Auto-tune and Speech coverage) and **Details** (titles, language, media type, series, status, episode, summary, link and tags, then the credits: author, studio, director, cast, genre, the cover and romanized names, Fill in details). Details holds one draft: switching tabs keeps what you typed, and a dot on the tab shows it holds unsaved edits. Novels and comics have only Add media and Details. A link can open one with `#/drama/<id>/source?tab=transcribe` (or `add`, `details`; the old `tools` and `credits` links land on Transcribe and Details). In every tab only the first card opens by itself; the others are folded and remember how you left them. When Transcribe uploads a picked audio file and the run is then refused, the file is kept, and Add media shows it as attached straight away.

On the Run tab, the language, Chinese script, Detect speakers, speaker counts, extra names and replacement prompt are saved with the title when you press Transcribe or Save options, so another browser and the Queue use the same choices; until then they are kept only in this browser. A line above the button lists the settings the run will use and says whether they are saved yet.

When Detect speakers with no count typed finds two or fewer speakers in a clip of three minutes or more, the finished job says "Found 2 speakers. If the audio has more, try raising the speaker count." (with the number it found); a crowded clip often has more speakers than the automatic count finds. Detect speakers runs on the CPU when the installed PyTorch has no kernels for your GPU (for example a V100 with the CUDA 12.8 build); install the CUDA 12.6 build under Diagnostics > GPU PyTorch to use the GPU. If the GPU crashes outright during a transcription, speaker detection, Re-transcribe (one line or the ticked lines) or Auto-tune run (on Windows, exit code 3221225477), the run starts again once on the CPU and the finished job says it ran on the CPU (the Auto-tune card says so too); a transcription that can resume (see above) continues on the CPU after the last two minutes the GPU finished instead of starting over; a transcription that uses Groq is not run again, so its Groq calls are not paid for twice. A detection run that finds no speech at all (silent or music-only audio) changes no speaker and ends with "Nothing was produced". With the Qwen3-ASR speech-detection backends, a failure in the forced aligner keeps the finished transcription with its estimated line times and the job reports that timing was not refined. A line the forced aligner could not time at all, or timed out of order, keeps its approximate window times and is flagged "Timing uncertain"; one fast character with no width no longer does that to the whole line.

Other transcribe options (Workspace > Transcribe > Run > Advanced options, folded into Speakers, Timing, Models and backends, Guards (Developer Mode) and Switches, which lists every on/off option and names the ones that are on when folded. Switches opens on a title whose options were never saved, and Speakers opens once they are; each fold remembers how you left it. "Extra names to expect" sits with the replacement prompt in the "More: replace the automatic prompt" fold, whose header says what is set. Ranges read like "0.1-2.0 s, default 0.35 s"):
- **Min silence** (ms; default 300, range 100-3000; lower values split at shorter pauses and can cut mid-sentence) and **VAD threshold** (the Silero speech-detection threshold, 0.1-0.9): the first controls how short a pause starts a new line, the second helps with quiet dialogue or noise producing phantom lines. A "Sensitivity" preset (normal / sensitive): sensitive lowers a VAD threshold you haven't changed from 0.5 to 0.35 (for the Qwen3 "detect speech first" recognizers, their own speech-detection threshold) and drops Whisper's repeat penalties; it leaves Min silence alone. It is also a series default. Try it first when quiet or short lines are missing. Whatever the settings, a line that spans a long stretch (6 s or more) with almost no text in it is heard again on its own and replaced when that finds more, because Whisper sometimes jumps over 20 seconds or more of fast or shouted speech.
- **Difficult audio** (Switches, off by default; also a series default): for speakers who scream, whisper or talk over each other, or when Sensitivity is not enough, where lines go missing or are misheard. It turns Whisper's speech detector off so Whisper hears the whole file (the detector scores shouted or slurred speech as "not speech"; Min silence and VAD threshold then don't apply to Whisper, and Whisper fast mode keeps the detector), transcribes a temporary levelled copy of the audio (needs ffmpeg; the stored file is never changed, and the job notes it if ffmpeg is missing) and makes Whisper less eager to drop low-confidence speech. It also sends Whisper a plain sentence instead of the series' names (Whisper otherwise echoes the name list as junk lines); names you type in the box or a prompt you set still apply. Re-hearing a gap or lines drops a result that is only a caption or credit such as "a few moments later" or "字幕by…". A line you re-transcribe (one, several, or Fix flagged) is heard the same way, without the detector. Slower, and it can add a few false lines; the usual repeat and hallucination filters still run, and a run of six or more short comma-separated items packed into a short stretch (invented lists of names) is dropped, while a lone list or a CJK line faster than 15 characters a second, a run of three or more one-character CJK lines, letters squeezed into under half a second, or any line under a third of a second is flagged "text_uncertain"; a credit line such as a channel name over the final outro is dropped for you to check; a credit or subscribe/like/share line that sits next to speech, and any line in Cyrillic or Greek, is kept but flagged "text_uncertain". Whisper and Qwen3-ASR (the Whisper-boundary one) use all three; the "detect speech first" Qwen3 recognizers hear the levelled audio in fixed 8-second windows with no speech detector, so quiet or shouted speech is no longer skipped, and the forced aligner places the lines (a few lines over silence can be invented, so check them; with Mixed languages on they keep their detector and only get the levelling); anime-whisper only gets the levelling; Groq and a supplied transcript ignore it. To test it on a clip first: `python -m tools.asr_bench TITLE_ID --config whisper:large-v3 --config whisper:large-v3,vad=sensitive --config whisper:large-v3,difficult=on`. With Developer Mode on, Settings > Timing and splitting also holds the two Whisper numbers it relaxes (the confidence floor is negative, for example -1.5).
- **Timing and splitting** (Settings > Performance and advanced). Shows the numbers that decide how long a line may run (default 8 s or 40 characters), how long a pause counts as a scene change when translating (3 s) and how fragments are joined into a sentence (1 s apart), each with its default, a plain tooltip and a Reset button. The speech-detection and tidy-up numbers behind them appear when Developer Mode is on, and read as their defaults when it is off. A change only affects runs you start afterwards; lines you already have are not touched.
- **Separate vocals first** (removes background music before transcribing): vocal separation with `audio-separator` (a Mel-Band RoFormer model; it needs the GPU switched on). Adds a full extra pass; skip it unless the background is music alone or the speech is whispered (in the benchmark it hurt with noise and did nothing on clean audio; on two hand-corrected whispered Chinese clips it lowered errors, but it can drop short fillers such as 嗯).
- **Realign long segments** (experimental, off by default): re-aligns a line against its own audio with Meta's MMS aligner (`pip install torchaudio uroman`, ~1.1GB model on first use). It only re-times text Whisper already produced.
- **Coverage checks.** Review's Look for problems group (it carries a count when there is something to look at) shows a "Coverage and pacing" section (long lines, large gaps, no source text, not translated) to look at before translating. Media > Transcribe has a separate "Speech coverage" > "Check coverage". For a title with burned-in captions in a speaker colour each, "Read on-screen captions by speaker" in Media > Transcribe (PC only; needs a picture-capable engine, shows the most it can cost, then confirm) reads them and sets each line's speaker from them; speakers you set by hand are kept. Afterwards you can type who each colour is (the title's series remembers it for its other videos) and press "Put speakers on lines", or "Find differences" to see lines where the caption is close to the transcript but spelled differently (a misheard name) and tick the ones that should take the caption's text.
- A translation that failed in the background shows as a banner in the Workspace until dismissed; press Translate again and already-translated lines are skipped.
- **Raw transcripts.** Every transcription run's untouched output is kept in the title's folder (`raw_transcript.json` for the first run, a timestamped file for each later one); Review's "what did transcription originally say" reads the newest. None is ever deleted: the first run and the newest 5 stay as plain JSON, and the runs in between are compressed in place to `.json.gz` (checked by reading it back before the plain file goes). A title with "Keep original files" on keeps every later run as plain JSON (runs compressed before it was turned on stay compressed).

### OCR (image-based chapter scans)

Upload page images in the novel-narration OCR section instead of pasting text. Always review OCR output before translating.
- **Tesseract** (used for languages other than ja, zh and ko when the OCR default is `auto`, the setting's default; ja picks manga-ocr and zh/ko pick RapidOCR): `pip install pytesseract pillow` plus the Tesseract binary with language packs (macOS `brew install tesseract tesseract-lang`; Ubuntu `sudo apt install tesseract-ocr tesseract-ocr-chi-sim tesseract-ocr-jpn tesseract-ocr-kor`; Windows [installer](https://github.com/UB-Mannheim/tesseract/wiki)). If Windows can't find it, set Settings > Performance and advanced > Offline, paths and limits > Program locations > "Tesseract program" to `tesseract.exe`.
- **RapidOCR** (install from Diagnostics): Chinese, Japanese and Korean, the Auto pick for Chinese and Korean pages and burned-in captions. Pages use its larger models, downloaded once on first use (about 135 MB, Korean about 13 MB); captions use the small built-in ones so a long video stays fast.
- **PaddleOCR** (`pip install paddleocr paddlepaddle`): heavier, better on stylized Chinese fonts.
- **manga-ocr** (`pip install manga-ocr`): Japanese speech bubbles; best on single-bubble crops.

### Hardsub OCR (captions burned into a video; experimental)

Choose "Read burned-in subtitles" as the transcript source (needs a video file). It samples frames, finds the caption row band, OCRs it with the same backends and collapses repeats into timed lines. Limits: one caption band only, a static logo can outscore a faint caption, and it is slower than audio transcription (raise the "Hardsub interval" to speed it up). For Chinese, Korean and Japanese titles the Hardsub OCR picker starts on Auto: RapidOCR, then PaddleOCR, then Tesseract only if neither is installed (Tesseract is a separate program you install yourself). The Hardsub OCR picker also offers Qwen3-VL (4B or 8B), a vision model that needs a CUDA GPU and a one-time Hugging Face download (about 9 GB for 4B, 17 GB for 8B); Auto never picks it, and if it can't load RapidOCR reads the rest of the run and the job says so. Cancel stops a Qwen3-VL run within the frame it is on (a slow frame is cut short), and captions read so far are not kept. Chinese captions read as Traditional are converted to Simplified when the title's script is Simplified (needs the optional OpenCC package).

### Simplified vs Traditional Chinese

For `zh` sources, the "Chinese script" select (Keep current, Simplified (Mainland), Traditional (Taiwan, Hong Kong); the default is Simplified) affects only OCR (Tesseract `chi_sim` vs `chi_tra`; install the matching language pack) and Reader word segmentation (Traditional is converted with OpenCC to find word boundaries, then the original text is sliced), and Whisper: Simplified or Traditional starts Whisper's prompt with a short sentence in that script ("以下是普通话的句子。" or "以下是普通話的句子。"), which keeps it from switching script and leaving out punctuation; a line that is only that sentence is dropped. Translation is unaffected.

**VTuber names (optional, personal use).** In a title's Glossary > Packs tab, *VTuber names* can download a list of VTuber names from the public dd-center/vdb database to this PC (about 2.4 MB, only when you press Download). It is under the VTuber Public Database International License v1.0 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 plus GPL text): it is for your own non-commercial use, Baihe never exports, backs up for sharing or uploads it, and it is not part of the app or installer. If you share it or anything made from it, credit dd-center/vdb and use the same licence. Switch it on per title (off by default): names found in that title's text then appear under *Find possible names* as *Known VTuber names* (you decide which become glossary terms) and are sent to the translator as "keep as written", never overriding a glossary term. Delete removes the file.

### Speaker diarization

Needs `pip install pyannote.audio soundfile`, a free Hugging Face token, and accepting the model terms at https://huggingface.co/pyannote/speaker-diarization-community-1. First run downloads the model. If Hugging Face refuses the token or the terms aren't accepted yet, the run says so in one sentence (the server log has Hugging Face's own reply). CPU works, GPU is faster.

**Speaker detection model** (Settings > System > Advanced) picks Nemotron-3 (the default) or pyannote community-1. Nemotron-3 is NVIDIA's newer model and mixed up fewer lines on the four titles we scored (those four only; on a 3 h and a 5.5 h file it ran without errors and about 6-8 times faster, but without corrected labels to score it against; not tried on a V100). It needs the `transformers` and `librosa` packages and downloads about 380 MB the first time you detect speakers. To go back, pick pyannote community-1 in the same place. A typed speaker count or range always uses pyannote, and a Nemotron run that fails (missing packages, no download) falls back to pyannote and the finished job says why. After a Nemotron run the voice-match suggestions still work, because the voice prints are made from the downloaded pyannote model (if that model isn't on the PC, a Nemotron run has no suggestions). Detection still needs the Hugging Face token because of that fallback. Each line gets the speaker heard for the most time in it, added up over all of that speaker's turns (so someone with several short turns in a line beats a single longer turn by someone else). A line Nemotron is unsure about (its confidence below 0.8) gets no speaker rather than a guess, so expect a few more blank speakers to fill in by hand and fewer wrong ones; pyannote runs and solo series are unaffected, and lines whose speaker you set by hand are kept unless you choose to replace them. Titles detected before this change keep their labels until you run Detect speakers again.

### Live (experimental)

The Live page pulls a running stream, cuts it into short chunks (your choice of length), transcribes and translates each in the background, and shows a growing feed. Needs `yt-dlp` and `ffmpeg`.

With no engine key saved, the engine box says **Add an engine key in Settings** (a link). If Ollama is missing it is shown as a note until you press Start.

The form's choices, limits and starting values come from the server, so Ollama starts on the default model set in Settings. Options you change are remembered in this browser; the rest follow the app's defaults.

- **Chunk length.** Latency is at least one chunk: a word at the start of a 20 s chunk waits the whole 20 s before Whisper hears it. Shorter chunks lower that but give Whisper less context. **Fast captions** (Advanced) sets a 4 s chunk, 1 s overlap, Whisper small and no thinking for sooner, rougher lines. It replaces the current values of those four options, which the browser then remembers; to go back, set them by hand (the app's defaults are chunk 20 s, overlap 3 s, Whisper small, no thinking).
- **Whisper model** (Advanced). small is the default and keeps up on most PCs. large-v3-turbo is slower but more accurate (on a 5-minute Chinese stream test, about 16 character errors per 100 against small's 23, in about 1.5 times the time on a CPU); it needs about 2.6 GB of graphics memory with Use GPU (about 2 GB of RAM without) and downloads about 1.6 GB the first time. medium was both slower and less accurate than turbo on that test. If the model is too slow for the chunk length, Live skips audio to catch up (below).
- **Translation runs beside Whisper.** Each chunk's lines go to the engine in one request while Whisper carries on with the next chunk. If the engine falls behind the stream, the oldest waiting lines are shown untranslated ("skipped to keep up") and counted, so captions stay close to the speech.
- **Video delay.** With **Automatic video delay** on (the default), the picture plays about one chunk plus the measured time to caption a chunk (the median of the last five) behind live, shown as "Video delay: auto (about N s)". Before the first chunk is measured it assumes 8 s of processing. Moving the slider sets the delay by hand and turns the automatic delay off. Where the stream's own player can't be held back, turn on **Delayed player** (off by default) before Start: Baihe records a delayed copy of the picture and sound on this PC and plays that instead (it downloads the video too); **Delayed player quality** picks 360p to 1080p (480p by default). On phone browsers that play the copy without reporting its length (some Android Chrome builds), the captions wait the chosen delay instead of showing at once.
- **Full screen.** With **Captions over video** on, use **Full screen with captions** under the video: the player's own full-screen button is hidden then, because it would show the picture without the captions. On a phone held upright, full screen turns the picture sideways. When the picture can't be held back far enough, a chunk's lines reach the overlay after they were spoken; they are then shown one after another at the pace they were spoken, starting when they arrive.
- **Caption lag.** "Captions about N s behind the stream" is how long after the first word of the newest captioned chunk was spoken its translation was ready: the chunk's own length plus its wait, Whisper and translation time. It is measured from when the audio reached Baihe, so the platform's own delay (often several seconds) comes on top.
- Whisper loads before capture starts, so the first chunk is not behind the stream. The status names each step and how fast the last chunk was (audio seconds, time taken, times real time).
- If Whisper is slower than the stream, the oldest waiting audio is skipped ("Skipped N s to catch up") instead of falling further behind. A chunk Whisper cannot finish in max(30 s, 6 x chunk length) is skipped ("Skipped chunk N") and the run goes on; while an abandoned Whisper call is still running, further chunks are skipped ("Whisper is still busy") instead of starting a second one. Skips are counted in one line per reason and stay listed under the status.
- **Stop** captions what was already captured (the chunks still waiting and the last partial chunk), lets their translations finish, then ends; the button then reads **Stop now**, which ends at once, within a couple of seconds even in the middle of a Whisper call. An error ends the session without captioning what was waiting. When **Stop after** runs out or the stream ends, the notes under the status say which.
- **Use glossary and packs from** (optional; None by default) picks a series or a title. The glossary always comes from a series: pick the series for its glossary and the language packs of its newest title that has a pack choice, or pick a title for its series' glossary and that title's own packs (a title with no series has no glossary). The names and terms that occur in a chunk are sent with that chunk's translation request. Nothing is written to the library.
- **Read chat** (Advanced; YouTube video and live links only) also reads the stream's live chat and shows it in a **Chat** panel under the lines, each message as written with its English translation beneath it, newest first, with a Super Chat's amount beside the name. It uses the same translator as the lines, so a paid engine bills the chat too and the monthly cap and the usage log cover it. To keep that small, messages with nothing to translate (emotes, numbers, links, one repeated character, a line another chatter just sent, text in the wrong script) show as written; when the chat runs ahead only the newest 40 of a poll are translated, in batches of 15; and a session translates at most 1,500 chat messages (a note says when it is reached). A chat that fails to translate does not end the session. It reads, never posts. A channel link such as `@name/live` has no video id, so use the watch link. Not tested against a real broadcast yet.
- Accuracy is lower than the normal pipeline: each chunk is transcribed alone with a few seconds of overlap, and Whisper gets no glossary priming.
- The feed is not saved to the Library; copy what you want before you stop.
- A resolved stream URL can expire after a few hours; stop and start again.
- Start says plainly when ffmpeg or yt-dlp is missing, and a stream link that won't open shows yt-dlp's own reason. After a reload this browser still shows the video of the session it started; a session started elsewhere (a followed channel, another device) shows its lines, and the video only with the Delayed player at the PC. If Start is refused because a session is already running, that session and its Stop appear.
- From another device, starting Live needs the **import from a link** permission (an admin account has it; a member gets it from an admin in People and access > People > Rights, or with `python -m api grant <email> media.import_url` at the PC), and a paid engine or an Ollama cloud model also needs **paid engines** (`engines.paid`). The note saying so shows only to an account that lacks it. **Delayed player** is not offered there, since it records on the PC, and a refusal for another reason shows that reason.
- Tested against a continuous ffmpeg capture, not a real live broadcast.

**Followed channels** (on the Live page). Paste a YouTube channel link (`https://www.youtube.com/@name`, `/channel/UC...`, `/c/name`), a Twitch channel link (`https://www.twitch.tv/name`) or an `@handle` (YouTube). A Twitch row's **Start Live** starts on the channel's page; Twitch has no scheduled streams to show, and ads in the stream are not filtered out. Checks are manual by default: **Check now** on a row, or **Check all my channels** (the PC owner: **Check all channels**), which checks them one after another. A row then reads "Live now: <title>" with a **Start Live** button (it starts Live with the channel's saved options, under the usual Live permissions; **Use Live options** saves the Live form's current ones), "Starts at HH:MM" in your own time for a scheduled stream, "Not live", or a short plain error. A channel can be checked once a minute, and only one check runs at a time on the PC; a manual check notifies no one. Household members can follow up to 5 channels each (20 in all on one PC); each person sees only their own, and the PC owner sees everyone's. A check only reads the channel's page (no video is downloaded). Saved yt-dlp cookies (Settings) are used only for the PC owner's own channels.
- **Check every hour** (per channel, off by default) checks the channel about once an hour (never more often than every 30 minutes), plus once about a minute after a stream scheduled within the hour starts, and sends one bell notification per stream. A failed hourly check (YouTube asking for a sign-in check, a private or members-only stream, no connection) makes it check less often, doubling up to every 6 hours until a check works again. Discord/ntfy alerts go out only for the PC owner's own channels.
- **Start Live automatically** is offered only to the PC owner, for their own channels, is off by default and needs **Check every hour** on (turning that off returns the channel to Notify me). It starts Live with that channel's saved options only when no other Live session and no GPU job is running and the monthly spending cap has room, stops at the saved Stop-after limit, and never starts the same stream twice. When it can't start, the channel's status says why ("not started: another Live session is running").

### Novel-narration line timing and EPUB

With no source audio, line timings come from each generated TTS clip, so download the `.srt` after generating the track. Novel-narration mode can import chapters from an `.epub` you own (needs `ebooklib`, `beautifulsoup4`) and export the translation as an `.epub`. Imported chapters saved as GBK, Big5, Shift-JIS or UTF-16 are read in that encoding. The exported `.epub` starts a chapter at each of the novel's own chapter headings (第一章, 第5話, 제3화, Chapter 2, Prologue and the like), named by the heading; a chapter over 200 lines continues as "(2)", "(3)", and a novel without headings is split every 200 lines. A title with no text in the chosen field (no translation yet) is refused instead of exported empty. Chapter OCR skips a page that isn't a readable image and counts it as skipped.

## Narration voices & voice cloning

- **Voice engine**: OmniVoice is not bundled; install it from its own "Voice cloning: OmniVoice" card in Diagnostics (it installs into a folder of its own, like Qwen3-TTS and Chatterbox). It clones a voice from a 3-10s clip or designs one from a description, and speaks Chinese, Japanese and Korean. A speaker with no clip gets a designed voice. "Find clips in the audio" pulls reference clips from a diarized audio drama. Not verified end to end in development: test on one short line first.
- **Qwen3-TTS** (Listen and Voices): clones a voice from a clip only (no designed voices), in Chinese, Japanese, Korean and English; give the clip's transcript for the best match. It lives in its own folder (`voice_engines/qwen3_tts` in the data folder) so its packages never change Baihe's own; until that folder is installed it shows "Qwen3-TTS is not installed. Install it in Diagnostics." The first narration downloads its model (a few GB). A narration speaks all of its Qwen3-TTS lines in one go, and a line it can't speak is left as a gap with "The voice engine could not speak this line."
- **Chatterbox** (Listen and Voices): clones a voice from a clip only, in Chinese, Japanese, Korean and English; it doesn't use the clip's transcript. Like Qwen3-TTS it lives in its own folder (`voice_engines/chatterbox`), shows "Chatterbox is not installed. Install it in Diagnostics." until that is installed, downloads its model on the first narration and speaks all of its lines in one go. Everything it speaks carries an inaudible watermark (Resemble AI's Perth). A character saved with the old Chatterbox engine uses this one.
- **Removed engines**: Edge TTS, Piper, F5-TTS, TADA and GPT-SoVITS were removed. A title or character that still names one shows a plain "removed" message and will not generate until you pick another engine; saved settings, clips and already generated dub tracks are kept. The old `library/piper_voices` folder is no longer used and can be deleted by hand (Library tools > Data folder lists it as "Old voices").
- **Voices**: Review > Change many lines > Voices shows each speaker's reference clip, voice bank entry and actor; a speaker whose chosen engine was removed shows a warning there. The separate Dub stage, which fitted a dubbed track to the original timing and mixed in the background, was removed; narration is made with Listen in the Reader, and Export > Audiobook builds the M4B. A title dubbed before then can still use Export > Video with the dub audio, and its old dub track plays as the narration in the Reader.
- **Narration**: consecutive lines from the same speaker in a paragraph are voiced in one call; each stays its own cue.
- **Audiobook export**: Export > Audiobook block > "Start audiobook export" builds an M4B with chapter markers from the novel's headings, Japanese (第5話, プロローグ) and Korean (제3화, 12화, 프롤로그) ones included (or one per paragraph).

Cloning real people's voices, even for personal fan translation, is a legal and ethical gray area; think it through before sharing dubbed files.
