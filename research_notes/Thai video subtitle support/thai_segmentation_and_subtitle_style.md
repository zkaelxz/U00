# Thai word segmentation and Thai subtitle line-splitting/timing rules (as of 2026-10-11)

## Thai word segmentation tools (PyThaiNLP engines, ICU / Intl.Segmenter): accuracy, speed, install footprint, licence, best lightweight default

### Takeaway
For a local Windows desktop app, the safe default is PyThaiNLP's `newmm` (or `newmm-safe`). Core PyThaiNLP 5.x is pure Python with almost no dependencies and an Apache-2.0 licence. Its known weakness is word-level accuracy (WL-F1 0.67 on BEST-2010, against 0.93 for DeepCut). Subtitles only need break points that do not cut through a word, and a dictionary maximal-matching engine bound by Thai character clusters almost never cuts mid-cluster, so that weakness costs little here. The neural engines (attacut, deepcut, oskut, sefr_cut) now all go through `lekcut`, which pulls in onnxruntime and numpy: more accurate, but heavier. PyICU publishes only an sdist and no Windows wheels, so it should not be a hard dependency. In the browser, `Intl.Segmenter('th', {granularity:'word'})` is dictionary-based (ICU4C) or LSTM-based (ICU4X) depending on the engine, and good enough for previewing wraps.

### Cited Findings
- PyThaiNLP `word_tokenize` engines: `newmm` (default; "dictionary-based maximal matching, constrained by Thai Character Cluster boundaries", described as a "balance between accuracy and efficiency"), `newmm-safe` (newmm "with a mechanism to avoid long processing time for text with continuously ambiguous breaking points"), `longest`, `mm`/multi_cut, `nercut` (newmm plus merging of named-entity tokens), `icu` (PyICU), `attacut`, `deepcut` ("user need to install deepcut (and its dependency: tensorflow)" in the 5.0 docs), `oskut`, `sefr_cut`, `tltk` ("maximum collocation approach") and `nlpo3` ("adaptation of newmm in Rust (2.5x faster)"). `custom_dict` works only with deepcut, longest, newmm and newmm-safe. — [PyThaiNLP 5.0 tokenize docs](https://pythainlp.org/docs/5.0/api/tokenize.html)
- The current dev registry also lists a `budoux` word engine and the alias `onecut` for newmm. — [pythainlp/tokenize/_registry.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyThaiNLP/pythainlp/dev/pythainlp/tokenize/_registry.py)
- PyThaiNLP latest is 5.4.0 (released 9 Oct 2026). It needs Python >=3.9 (classifiers 3.9–3.14). Code is Apache-2.0; data and models are CC0-1.0 / CC-BY-4.0. The `compact` extra installs "a stable and small subset of dependencies"; `full` installs "all optional dependencies (may introduce conflicts)". — [PyPI pythainlp](https://pypi.org/project/pythainlp/)
- Core dependencies in pyproject are only `importlib_resources` (Python <3.11) and `tzdata` (Windows). Extras: `attacut`, `oskut` and `sefr_cut` each map to `lekcut>=1.0.0`; `icu` → `pyicu>=2.3`; `nlpo3` → `nlpo3>=1.4.0`; `budoux` → `budoux>=0.7.0`. — [PyThaiNLP pyproject.toml (dev)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyThaiNLP/pythainlp/dev/pyproject.toml)
- LEKCut 1.0.0 (Apache-2.0) "ports the deep learning model to the onnx model". Its models are deepcut (default), attacut-sc, attacut-c, oskut (with engine variants ws/tnhc/scads/...) and sefr-best/sefr-tnhc/sefr-ws1000. It requires numpy, onnxruntime, onnx, ssg, python-crfsuite and pyahocorasick, so TensorFlow and torch are no longer needed for these engines. — [PyPI lekcut JSON](https://pypi.org/pypi/lekcut/json)
- PyICU 2.16.2 on PyPI ships only `pyicu-2.16.2.tar.gz` (no wheels, so it needs a compiler and an ICU install on Windows). — [PyPI pyicu JSON](https://pypi.org/pypi/pyicu/json)
- The PyThaiNLP paper says a pure-Python NewMM was written for "easy installation and consistent behavior across platforms", because the earlier implementation needed marisa-trie, which "does not officially support mingw32 compiler on Windows". It reports that newmm "is the fastest word tokenizer on the BEST 2010 benchmark, with 71.18 % accuracy (compared to state-of-the-art at 95.60 %)", and that NewMM was ported to Rust (nlpO3) for speed. — [PyThaiNLP paper, arXiv 2312.04649](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.04649)
- AttaCut paper, word-level F1 (WLF1, mean ± sd). BEST-2010: PyThaiNLP newmm 0.67, Sertis 0.87, DeepCut 0.93, AttaCut-C 0.89, AttaCut-SC 0.91. TNHC (classical literature): 0.73 / 0.70 / 0.63 / 0.66 / 0.63. Wisesight-1000 (social media): 0.74 / 0.81 / 0.81 / 0.80 / 0.81. Notably, "PyThaiNLP is the best word segmenter on TNHC", so the neural models do not generalise well out of domain. — [AttaCut, arXiv 1911.07056](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.07056)
- AttaCut speed on Wisesight train (2.15M chars). DeepCut took about 846 s on AWS t2.small and 522 s on t2.medium. AttaCut-SC was 9.1× / 5.6× faster than DeepCut, and AttaCut-C 14.3× / 9.9× faster. "PyThaiNLP is the fastest ... taking less than 10 seconds ... however, it suffers from low segmentation quality." Parameters: AttaCut-SC 158,993; AttaCut-C 173,533; DeepCut about 500K. BEST-2010 has 5.11M words and 18.74M chars from news, articles, encyclopedias and novels. — [AttaCut, arXiv 1911.07056](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.07056)
- AttaCut authors: "measuring only the character-level metrics would overestimate the tokenization performance". Papers With Code lists AttaCut-SC at F1 0.9839 on BEST-2010, which is likely character-level. — [AttaCut paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.07056); [Papers With Code](https://www.paperswithcode.com/paper/attacut-a-fast-and-accurate-neural-thai-word-segmenter)
- PyThaiNLP has `display_cell_tokenize(text)`, which splits Thai into display cells without separating tone marks or above/below vowels from their base. Example: `"แม่น้ำอยู่ที่ไหน"` → `['แ','ม่','น้ํ','า','อ','ยู่','ที่','ไ','ห','น']`. It decomposes ำ to ํ+า and attaches U+0E31, U+0E34–U+0E3A and U+0E47–U+0E4E to the previous cell. — [pythainlp/tokenize/core.py (dev)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyThaiNLP/pythainlp/dev/pythainlp/tokenize/core.py)
- ICU "provides dictionary support for word boundaries in Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese"; "Use of the dictionaries is automatic". The word lists live in `icu4c/source/data/brkitr/dictionaries`. ICU gives no accuracy figures. — [ICU User Guide: Boundary Analysis](https://unicode-org.github.io/icu/userguide/boundaryanalysis/)
- ICU4X WordSegmenter's automatic mode uses dictionaries for Chinese/Japanese and LSTM for Burmese, Khmer, Lao and Thai. A dictionary-only constructor is also available. — [ICU4X WordSegmenter docs](https://icu4x.unicode.org/1_5/tsdoc/classes/WordSegmenter.html)
- Firefox's `Intl.Segmenter` tests show ICU4C and ICU4X splitting Thai differently. For "พนักงานนําโคลงเรือสามตัว", ICU4C gives "พนัก|งาน|นํา|โคลง|เรือ|สาม|ตัว" and ICU4X gives "พ|นัก|งานนํา|โคลง|..."; the linguistically expected answer is "พนักงาน|นํา|โค|ลง|เรือ|สาม|ตัว". The test header says "Word boundaries are locale independent", so the `'th'` locale does not change results. — [Firefox word.js test](https://kdab.searchfox.org/firefox-main/source/js/src/tests/non262/Intl/Segmenter/word.js)

### Inferences
- Recommended default: `pythainlp.tokenize.word_tokenize(text, engine="newmm-safe")`. It is pure Python and Windows-safe, and the safe variant protects against pathological ASR strings. Register `pythainlp` in `diagnostics.OPTIONAL_DEPENDENCIES` and import it lazily. Treat `nlpo3` (Rust wheels) and the `lekcut` engines (onnxruntime, about tens of MB) as optional upgrades, never as install requirements. Do not use the `full` extra.
- For line breaking, the important property is never cutting inside a Thai Character Cluster (TCC) or syllable; exact word accuracy matters less. A safe fallback if PyThaiNLP is missing is to break only at existing spaces and, failing that, at grapheme-cluster boundaries that are not before a combining mark. Breaking inside a word is a style fault, but it does not corrupt rendering.
- In the React editor, `Intl.Segmenter('th', {granularity: 'word'})` is a zero-dependency way to preview line breaks. Its output may differ between Chromium (V8 + ICU4C dictionary) and Firefox (ICU4X), so the server-side (Python) split should be the authority.
- None of the benchmarks measure conversational or subtitle-domain Thai. Wisesight-1000 (social media) is the closest, and there newmm scores WLF1 0.74 against about 0.81 for the neural models.

### Gaps
- No primary benchmark with all of these engines (newmm, longest, icu, nercut, tltk, oskut, sefr_cut, budoux, nlpo3) on BEST-2010 or Orchid under one protocol was found. The PyThaiNLP benchmark visualisation page (pythainlp.github.io/tokenization-benchmark-visualization) was not fetched. The 71.18% vs 95.60% figure in the PyThaiNLP paper uses a different metric from the AttaCut WLF1 table.
- OSKut and SEFR-CUT accuracy figures (from their own papers) were not retrieved, and the TLTK licence was not checked.
- Which ICU build or engine Chrome and Edge use for Thai `Intl.Segmenter` in 2026 (ICU4C dictionary is the assumption) was not confirmed from source. Whether Firefox has fully switched Thai to the ICU4X LSTM model is inferred from the test comments only.
- Install size of onnxruntime on Windows was not measured.

## Rules for breaking a Thai subtitle line

### Takeaway
Break only at word boundaries, and preferably at the existing phrase spaces, which in Thai play the role of commas and full stops. Never separate a combining mark (above/below vowels U+0E31 and U+0E34–U+0E3A, tone marks and other marks U+0E47–U+0E4E) from its base. Keep ๆ attached to the preceding word: it must not start a line on its own. Keep closing quotes, brackets, ?, ! and % off the start of a line, keep opening quotes and brackets off the end of a line, and keep ฿ with its number. Leading vowels (เ แ โ ใ ไ) are spacing characters that begin a syllable, so a break may fall before them but never directly after them. This follows from Thai orthography; none of the sources fetched states it as a rule. Netflix wants one line where possible, otherwise two lines with a bottom-heavy pyramid.

### Cited Findings
- "When Thai text is wrapped at the end of a line it should break at word boundaries." Applications use dictionary lookup, which can fail on compounds and proper names, and authors can insert ZWSP (U+200B) or WJ (U+2060) to control breaks. Browser heuristics give "no guarantee" for other languages written in Thai script. — [r12a Thai orthography notes](https://r12a.github.io/scripts/thai/th.html)
- Line-edge rules: opening quotes and brackets "should not be the last character on a line". Closing quotes and brackets, and the ?, !, section and % marks "should not begin a new line". The baht sign ฿ "should be kept with any number, even if separated by a space or parenthesis". The repetition mark ๆ "shouldn't be wrapped to the beginning of a new line on its own". — [r12a Thai orthography notes](https://r12a.github.io/scripts/thai/th.html)
- Thai uses spaces to separate phrases, not words, often where English would use a comma or full stop. Traditional texts may use a small space (width of ก) and a larger one (double that). — [r12a Thai orthography notes](https://r12a.github.io/scripts/thai/th.html); [W3C thai-lreq](https://www.w3.org/TR/thai-lreq/)
- A grapheme cluster is a base, optional sara am, then combining marks; non-combining vowels are separate clusters. With `line-break: anywhere`, Gecko, WebKit and Blink "all wrap on grapheme cluster boundaries". Thai justification "primarily adjusts the blank spaces between phrases", and ZWSP used to separate words is ignored during justification. — [r12a Thai orthography notes](https://r12a.github.io/scripts/thai/th.html)
- "Only the vowel sign components that appear above or below the consonant are combining marks"; other vowel components are spacing letters "typed in the order seen". The W3C thai-lreq document itself is still a gap-analysis questionnaire (§8.1 line breaking) and states no concrete rules. — [W3C thai-lreq](https://www.w3.org/TR/thai-lreq/)
- Netflix Thai: "Maximum two lines"; "Keeping the translation on one line is preferred"; "Line breaks are allowed when the translation exceeds the limitation, or when it may be confusing for the audience"; "Prefer a bottom-heavy pyramid shape"; avoid "having just one or two words on the top line". Dual speakers: "Use a hyphen followed by a space ... with a maximum of one speaker per line." — [Netflix Thai Timed Text Style Guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/220448308-Thai-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)
- Netflix Thai spacing and punctuation: "Sentences are to be separated with a single space"; no periods as terminal punctuation; commas only in lists of names; ellipsis is the single character U+2026; repetition mark ๆ takes "only one space after the mark, and not before" and is not used "for sentences and long phrases"; no space before "!"; no italics. — [Netflix Thai Timed Text Style Guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/220448308-Thai-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)

### Inferences
- Splitter algorithm. (1) Normalise: NFC, collapse runs of spaces, keep a single space as the phrase separator. (2) If the text fits within the CPL limit (see below), use one line. (3) Otherwise choose the break candidate nearest the middle, biased so the bottom line is longer, in this order: existing spaces, then word boundaries from newmm, then grapheme-cluster boundaries as a last resort. (4) Reject any candidate that would put ๆ, a closing bracket or quote, ?, !, % or a combining mark at the start of a line, or an opening bracket or quote or ฿ at the end. (5) At a space break, drop the space; at a word break, insert nothing (no hyphen).
- Because Netflix does not use full stops in Thai, ASR or LLM output with "." or "," should be converted to a single space when producing Thai subtitles.
- Bilingual Thai+English layout (Thai over English, each in its own line block) is common practice, but no primary style guide covering it was found. Treat each language block with its own CPL limit.

### Gaps
- No primary source spells out a rule that a line must not start with a leading vowel or end with one. This is inferred from orthography: a leading vowel belongs to the following consonant. Paiyannoi ฯ and ฯลฯ line-edge behaviour is not covered by Netflix or r12a in what was fetched.
- No primary source was found on bilingual Thai/English subtitle layout norms (Thai OTT, Viki, YouTube).
- An initial fetch summary of the Netflix Thai guide said it disallows question marks and dashes. A second, more targeted fetch did not repeat this, so it should be re-read on the page before being enforced.

## Thai subtitle limits (CPL, lines, CPS, duration, gap) and how characters are counted

### Takeaway
Netflix Thai: 35 characters per line, not counting tone marks and above/below vowels (count base or spacing characters only); at most 2 lines; reading speed 17 CPS for adult and 13 for children (SDH 20 and 17). General Netflix timing: minimum 5/6 s (20 frames at 24 fps), maximum 7 s, minimum gap 2 frames. Next to the known Netflix numbers (ja 13/4, ko 16/12, zh 16/9, en 42/20), Thai is th 35 CPL / 17 CPS, much closer to English than to CJK.

### Cited Findings
- Netflix Thai subtitles: "35 characters per line (excluding all composite characters, i.e. tone marks, top and bottom vowels are not counted)". "Maximum two lines." Reading speed up to 17 characters per second for adult programs and 13 for children's programs. SDH: 35 CPL, 2 lines, 20 CPS adult and 17 children ("speaker IDs and sound labels are included"). The font section uses Arial as a placeholder, with size "relative to video resolution and ability to fit 35 characters across the screen". The page's change log was last updated 2025-10-17. — [Netflix Thai Timed Text Style Guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/220448308-Thai-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)
- Netflix Thai numbers: one to ten written out in words, numerals above 10, numerals for scores, dates and times; do not convert ค.ศ. to พ.ศ.; Arabic digits are used in the examples, and Thai digits (๐–๙) are not mentioned. — [Netflix Thai Timed Text Style Guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/220448308-Thai-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)
- Netflix general requirements: "Minimum duration: 5/6 (five-sixths) of a second per subtitle event (e.g. 20 frames for 24fps)"; "Maximum duration: 7 seconds per subtitle event"; "2 lines maximum"; centre-justified, at the top or bottom of the screen. — [Netflix General Requirements](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/215758617-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-General-Requirements)
- Netflix timing: "Subtitles must have a minimum of 2 frames between them" at any frame rate. Out-time should ideally be at least half a second (12 frames at 24 fps) past the end of the audio. In-time on the first frame of audio, or within 1–2 frames of it. Gaps of 3–11 frames at 24 fps are closed to 2 frames. Shot-change rules: snap to the cut when within 12 frames; dialogue that crosses a cut ends 2 frames before it or at least 12 frames after it. — [Netflix Subtitle Timing Guidelines](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051554394-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-Subtitle-Timing-Guidelines)
- Display-cell counting matches the Netflix rule. PyThaiNLP's `display_cell_tokenize` attaches U+0E31, U+0E34–U+0E3A and U+0E47–U+0E4E to the base character, so `len(display_cell_tokenize(s))` counts the characters Netflix counts. — [pythainlp/tokenize/core.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyThaiNLP/pythainlp/dev/pythainlp/tokenize/core.py)

### Inferences
- Counting function, with no dependency needed: count code points in `s` that are not in {U+0E31, U+0E34–U+0E3A, U+0E47–U+0E4E}. Sara am (U+0E33) is a spacing vowel and so counts as 1, though PyThaiNLP decomposes it into nikhahit + sara aa, which also gives 1 counted cell. Whether spaces count is not stated for Thai; Netflix practice in other languages counts spaces, so count them. This is not grapheme-cluster counting: grapheme clusters would merge sara am into the previous cell and treat leading vowels separately, which gives slightly different numbers.
- Suggested app constants: `th: max_cpl=35, max_lines=2, max_cps=17 (13 for kids), min_dur=0.833 s, max_dur=7 s, min_gap=2 frames`. CPS should use the same display-cell count.

### Gaps
- No primary BBC, YouTube, Viki or Thai-broadcaster (NBTC, Thai PBS) Thai subtitle limits were found in this pass. BBC subtitle guidelines are English-focused and were not checked for Thai.
- Netflix does not say whether spaces count toward the Thai 35 CPL, or whether Thai CPS excludes combining marks (presumably the same counting rule applies).

## Font and rendering issues for burned-in (libass/ffmpeg) and SRT/VTT subtitles

### Takeaway
libass has required HarfBuzz since 0.15.0, so current ffmpeg/libass builds shape Thai and position marks through the font's GPOS/GSUB tables. Most "stacked" or "floating" tone-mark bugs come from a missing Thai font (tofu or fallback) or from renderers that skip GPOS. For burn-in, ship or select an OFL Thai font explicitly (Noto Sans Thai, Noto Sans Thai Looped, Sarabun) in the ASS style, and use a font that also covers Latin for bilingual lines.

### Cited Findings
- libass 0.15.0 changelog: "Require HarfBuzz unconditionally to ensure good shaping for complex scripts". Earlier releases added "OpenType shaping via HarfBuzz-ng" and "Properly handle script and language in the HarfBuzz shaper". A later release added `ASS_FEATURE_WHOLE_TEXT_LAYOUT` "to apply bidi, shaping and \fay" across the whole text, since by default libass runs "bidi and shaping on each glyph run separately". — [libass Changelog](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/libass/libass/master/Changelog)
- Google Fonts metadata: Sarabun (designer Suppakit Chalermlarp), Noto Sans Thai and Noto Sans Thai Looped are all licensed OFL and include the `thai` and `latin` subsets. — [Sarabun METADATA](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/sarabun/METADATA.pb); [Noto Sans Thai METADATA](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/notosansthai/METADATA.pb); [Noto Sans Thai Looped METADATA](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/notosansthailooped/METADATA.pb)
- Tone marks need different placement when stacked over an upper vowel than over a bare consonant (GNOME/Pango Thai shaping patch). Detached marks in other renderers are attributed to not applying GPOS. — [GNOME bug 378001](https://bugzilla.gnome.org/show_bug.cgi?id=378001); [Figma forum report](https://forum.figma.com/report-a-problem-6/thai-script-rendering-broken-on-frame-names-vowels-and-tone-marks-detached-from-consonants-51323)
- Netflix Thai names only "Arial as a generic placeholder for proportional SansSerif". — [Netflix Thai Timed Text Style Guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/220448308-Thai-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)

### Inferences
- For ffmpeg burn-in: put the font file in a directory passed with `subtitles=...:fontsdir=<dir>` and name it in the ASS `Style` (e.g. `Noto Sans Thai`). Do not rely on system fallback, because Arial on Windows does not contain Thai and Windows' Thai fallback (Tahoma or Leelawadee UI) varies. Check the ffmpeg build's libass version (>=0.15 guarantees HarfBuzz). Thai marks above and below need extra vertical room, so consider a larger MarginV or line spacing.
- Tahoma ships with Windows and covers Thai, but it is not redistributable. Prefer bundling OFL fonts (Noto Sans Thai and Sarabun permit redistribution).
- In SRT/VTT played by browsers or players, shaping is done by the platform text stack (HarfBuzz, DirectWrite or CoreText), so Thai normally renders correctly when a Thai font is present. Editor text-width measurement should use display cells (above), not `str.length`.

### Gaps
- No specific open libass GitHub issue about Thai mark stacking was found; searches surfaced only non-libass reports. Whether particular ffmpeg Windows builds (gyan.dev, BtbN) ship libass with HarfBuzz was not checked, though the 0.15+ requirement implies it.
- Tahoma's licence and redistribution terms, and the exact Windows Thai fallback chain, were not verified from a primary source.

## Pre-processing Thai ASR text before Thai→English translation

### Takeaway
No Thai-specific primary source on ASR punctuation restoration was found. Practical steps, inferred: normalise Unicode (NFC; fix the common decomposed sara am and doubled tone marks, e.g. with PyThaiNLP's normaliser); restore phrase spaces or sentence breaks so each segment is a complete clause; map Thai digits ๐–๙ to Arabic digits; and send the LLM id-keyed whole sentences rather than fragments.

### Cited Findings
- Token-level punctuation restoration "cannot be applied directly to scriptio continua languages like Chinese, Japanese, and Thai". A character-level BiLSTM that restores punctuation, capitalisation and spaces was shown on Japanese and Gujarati (overall F 0.90), not on Thai. — [ICNLSP 2022 paper](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix_video/2022.icnlsp-1.19.pdf)
- PyThaiNLP has a sentence tokenizer (`crfcut`, a CRF trained "on translated TED transcripts"), plus `whitespace`, `tltk`, `thaisum` and `wtp*` engines. CRF-based sentence splitting is the most relevant for unpunctuated ASR text. — [PyThaiNLP paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.04649); [registry](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyThaiNLP/pythainlp/dev/pythainlp/tokenize/_registry.py)
- Netflix Thai uses spaces, not full stops, to separate sentences, and Arabic numerals (words for 1–10). — [Netflix Thai Timed Text Style Guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/220448308-Thai-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)

### Inferences
- For translation: merge ASR segments into sentence-sized units (`sent_tokenize(engine="crfcut")`, which comes with core PyThaiNLP since python-crfsuite is bundled; verify this), translate by id, then re-split the English to the original timing. For display: render Thai with phrase spaces and no full stops.
- Convert Thai digits for the MT input. `pythainlp.util.thai_digit_to_arabic_digit` exists in PyThaiNLP (from memory; verify). Keep พ.ศ. years as they are, and let the translator handle the conversion (พ.ศ. minus 543 = CE) only if asked.

### Gaps
- No primary evaluation of Thai ASR punctuation or space restoration models was found. Whether Whisper-family Thai output already includes phrase spaces was not verified.
- Whether `crfcut` needs the `python-crfsuite` package in PyThaiNLP 5.4 (it is not in core dependencies) was not confirmed, and it is possibly not installed by default. Check before relying on it.
